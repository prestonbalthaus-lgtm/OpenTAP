## Part 0. What the pivot resolved, and the findings that reshape it

The revision-3 charter removed the partner from the acknowledgement path. Three consequences run through every part of this document. First, the arena is the only copy of an acknowledged write until the Consistency Point commits, so the CP becomes the unit of durability, replication, snapshots and rollback, and "one CP window of acknowledged writes" is the loss figure for every unplanned event: a dirty boot on one node, or a promotion of a replica on two. Second, the sync-era machinery (`MirrorLink`, `MirrorInbound`, `BatchPage`, the `Placed` acknowledgement, `Credit`, the `GrantUnmirrored` hysteresis, the tmpfs inbound ring and `otap-ringd`) has no job left and is deleted; its 8 GiB goes to the read cache and the CP staging pool. Third, the SVM is the unit of everything: of namespaces, configuration, identity, replication consistency (the root volume ships with its data volumes) and mobility; the aggregate remains the unit of CP, and of media ownership only in Phase A.

Ten findings from this round reshape the design beyond what the charter asked:

1. The 6.12 ublk data path as the personas first wrote it does not exist: `READ_FIXED` on `/dev/ublkcN` fails with `-EACCES`, and a plain read is punted to a worker unless the fd is `O_NONBLOCK` (C03, C04). The corrected path is `IORING_OP_READ`/`WRITE` with a plain pointer into the arena slot, the fd set non-blocking before registration, and an exit test that the `io_uring_queue_async_work` tracepoint never fires. Legacy copy stays as the measured alternative.
2. Hardware RAID virtual drives hide their caches from the kernel in every configuration (C05 to C08): WCE=0 is presented regardless of policy, the kernel strips every flush and FUA, and `megaraid_sas` never lets a host flush reach the member drives. Durability on a PERC or MegaRAID VD therefore rests on an operator attestation that covers the controller cache, the member-drive caches and the learn mode, and on a `flush-opaque` handling that keeps CP N-2 intact. The reference configuration becomes an IT-mode HBA (or a PERC in HBA mode) with mdadm, where the kernel's flushes and FUA reach the drives.
3. ESXi declares Permanent Device Loss only when every path answers with a PDL sense; a dead path to a powered-off node keeps the device in All-Paths-Down and the VMs resume silently when the LUN returns (C01, C02). nvfail for block therefore needs either a changed device identity or the survivor answering on the dead node's path; LIO's virtual LUN 0 means data LUNs live at 1 to 255. ALUA Unavailable is never a PDL presentation.
4. The replication stream has one owner for the envelope and one for the payload: the chunk is the engine's self-describing stripe image, deterministic for a frozen generation, and the transport owns the 64-byte frame, the cursor, the credits and the connections. TLS is mandatory (R21), which removes zero copy from the arena and three kernel-version hazards with it.
5. Catch-up ships snapshot-to-snapshot increments in order, never a compacted walk across a retained snapshot, otherwise the replica's snapshots differ from the sender's (the SnapMirror and OpenZFS rule). A random 64-bit CP nonce distinguishes a re-issued CP sequence number after a sender crash.
6. The takeover gate becomes a posture, not a veto: the survivor always promotes, and lag decides whether each volume is held (acknowledging nothing until an operator clears), fenced (served at once under a new identity) or served as is. A held promotion is reversible if the old owner returns intact. The irreversible step is `nvfail clear`, where the four-fact printout lives.
7. SVM namespaces are three (network, UTS, mount), pinned from the host mount namespace by a helper that stays there, because a process cannot bind its own mount namespace file (C16); systemd 257 has no directive that joins a mount or UTS namespace by path (C15), so transient units enter through a wrapper. One `lockd` thread serves every SVM's NLM on 6.12 (C13); that is a documented limit, not a design choice.
8. Block protocols are SVM protocols: iSCSI portals bind in the writer's network namespace (C11), so each SVM carries its target on both nodes with one block LIF per node, and every block cutover is an ALUA flip. Block LIFs never move; NAS LIFs move with the SVM. Phase B therefore has standby paths, which the round-2 "byte-identical trees" ruling already assumed.
9. The plans the four personas wrote for Phase C are Phase C, B and 3 designs with Phase C labels: 38 work items, most needing an ESXi, Proxmox, Windows, Active Directory, Veeam or QLogic lab. Phase C as built is a 12-week, two-engineer, single-node iSCSI and NFSv3 appliance on mdadm or one NVMe, with crash-consistent snapshots, nvfail and file-based delta dump and restore, every format frozen in week 1 with reserved space for everything deferred.
10. Two things the homelab will not accept: a `strict` identity change as the default for LUNs a Linux host uses (it breaks Proxmox volume IDs and LVM bases), and a single-node product with no way to make `fsync` mean anything. The first is ruled per host class; the second brings the per-aggregate PLP-NVMe durable log back as the first post-v0.1 item, with its format reserved now.

What stands unchanged from round 2 is listed in the charter's section 2 and is not repeated; where this document is silent, round 2's ruling applies.

## Part 1. Opus-Storage (single-node engine, async delta shipper, nvfail)

### 1.1 Executive take

The arena is the only copy of an acknowledged write until the CP commits; the CP is the unit of durability, replication, snapshots and rollback; the partner is a consumer that runs behind the CP. The acknowledgement is a local insert on a reactor, with nothing on the data path waiting for a network. The arena is sized by exposure (about 10 s of ingest), not by link arithmetic, and nvfail stops being a dual-power-loss special case and becomes the engine's standard answer to any boot or promotion that is not at the acknowledged state. Phase C's real novelty is portability: the flusher must find full stripes on a PERC virtual drive, an LSI IR volume, an mdadm RAID6 and a lone NVMe, and must know which of them hides its write cache.

### 1.2 Libraries and kernel interfaces

Crates (2026-10-12 snapshot, all present at the named version per C39): io-uring 0.7.15 (`SINGLE_ISSUER`, `DEFER_TASKRUN`; the crate also builds `ReadvFixed`/`WritevFixed`, which 6.12 rejects), libublk 0.4.8 pinned exactly and vendored (nine of twenty-one releases yanked; its `sys` module advertises `UBLK_U_CMD_UPDATE_SIZE`, absent from 6.12), crc-fast 1.10.0, zerocopy 0.8.62 (pin `~0.8`), rustix 1.1.5, memmap2 0.9.11, crossbeam 0.8.5 (epoch pins for the cut), blake3 1.8.7, postcard 1.1.3, proptest 1.12.0, toml 1.1.8, roaring 0.11.5 as the `VolBitSet` candidate.

Kernel interfaces, verified on the v6.12 tag and Debian's 6.12.111-1: `UBLK_F_USER_COPY` (1 << 7) with `UBLKSRV_IO_BUF_OFFSET` 0x80000000 and the 25/16/12-bit offset, tag and queue fields; `UBLK_ATTR_VOLATILE_CACHE` and `UBLK_ATTR_FUA` in the basic params; `UBLK_IO_OP_FLUSH`; `UBLK_IO_F_META` (1 << 11); `USER_RECOVERY` and `REISSUE`; `ublks_max` as a runtime module parameter bounded by `UBLK_MINORS` (C10); no `UPDATE_SIZE` and no `REGISTER_IO_BUF` before 6.15 and 6.16. `IORING_OP_READ`/`WRITE`/`WRITE_FIXED`/`FSYNC`, `RWF_DSYNC` producing one `REQ_FUA` bio on the io_uring path with no trailing `generic_write_sync` (block/fops.c, io_uring/rw.c). md: `raid5_set_limits` (`io_min` = chunk, `io_opt` = chunk times data disks), `R5_WantFUA` propagation to members including parity, `md_flush_request` issuing empty `REQ_PREFLUSH` per member while a data-carrying PREFLUSH proceeds and member flush errors are only logged (C22). sd: `sd_set_flush_flag` (write cache iff WCE, FUA iff WCE and DPOFUA), `vpd_pgb0` as a read-only attribute (C35). `BLKIOMIN`, `BLKIOOPT`, `BLKALIGNOFF`, `BLKPBSZGET`, `BLKSSZGET`, `BLKDISCARD`, `FIFREEZE`, `FITHAW`. dm `log-writes` 1.1.0 and `flakey` 1.5.0 in-box.

### 1.3 Architecture

**Write path.** Reactors per core on io_uring; one ublk device per LUN with `USER_COPY`. The reactor that owns the LUN's queues takes the WRITE CQE, reserves a slot run in the arena, issues `IORING_OP_READ` on the `O_NONBLOCK` ublkc fd at the user-copy position into the slot (never `IOSQE_ASYNC`, `addr = 0` in every FETCH), computes CRC32C per 4 KiB, takes the aggregate `seq`, inserts into the volume's dirty index and issues `COMMIT_AND_FETCH_REQ`, which is the acknowledgement. Busy-polled cost per 4 KiB: 2.5 to 5.3 us; 8 to 13 us with wake-ups; 100 to 150 us p50 at an iSCSI initiator on 10GbE; 150 to 250 us in a guest. `dbladed` holds `CAP_SYS_ADMIN` only; `UBLK_F_UNPRIVILEGED_DEV` is excluded (C04).

**Arena, generations, triggers, back-pressure.** The arena is 8 GiB of boot-reserved 2 MiB pages in a hugetlbfs file created by systemd units and re-attached after `kill -9`; no holder process exists. The dirty index is one `BTreeMap<u64, DirtyExtent>` per volume per generation (about 80 B per entry; a generation is at most 160 MiB). Generations are index objects in a ring of eight; a cut is the epoch flip alone (grace under 50 us) and never waits for a flush; each closed generation becomes one CP in order. `cut_within(deadline)` is the promise the NAS snapshot scheduler needs before `FIFREEZE`. Triggers in priority: `Shutdown` and `UpsOnBattery`; `Snapshot(id)`; `ArenaHalf` (4 GiB unflushed); `Timer` (10 s HDD, 5 s flash); `BackToBack` (previous CP committed, above 1 GiB unflushed); `ShipApply` (receiver). The effective interval is min(timer, 4 GiB divided by ingest): 4 s at 1 GB/s. Back-pressure above 60% occupancy: `delay = k x occ / (1 - occ)`, `k` from the last three drain rates, capped at 2 s per request, then ALUA Transitioning (R7). An 80%-full HDD pool drains 0.3 to 0.5 GB/s; a 4 GiB burst at 2 GB/s is absorbed in 2 s.

**Stripe format, flush, commit.** A generation drains volume by volume in LBA order into stripe images in the hugetlbfs staging pool: `[frame header][summary][data]`. The summary is a 64 B header `{agg, cp_seq, cp_nonce, lseg, stripe_idx, kind, group, nblocks, crc}` plus 16 B per block `{vol:20 | lba:40 | kind:2 | rsvd:2, birth: u32, crc32c: u32}`, 0.5% of a 2.5 MiB HDD stripe. `kind` is `Ingest | Relocate | Applied | Merge`; stripes are homogeneous in kind and ingest is packed per consistency group, so only ingest stripes of outbound groups ship. Staging costs one copy (0.12 core at 1.2 GB/s) and buys one `WRITE_FIXED` per stripe, in-place patch merges and one plaintext source for the shipper. Per CP: data stripes as O_DIRECT `WRITE_FIXED`, `inflight_stripes` deep (measured drain times 200 ms, clamped to 4 to 64); the metadata stream (CP log at 16 B per block, table deltas, map checkpoint pages when due) packed into whole stripes of log-structured metadata segments on the metadata class, else the pool; `IORING_OP_FSYNC` per device class in order (pool, then metadata), each awaited; then the superblock slot written whole with `RWF_DSYNC`. The commit is "empty flush, wait, FUA record", never a data-carrying PREFLUSH (C22). On parity pools every write is a whole stripe, data and metadata alike; the four superblock slots and the two intent slots are each one full stripe written whole with FUA (15 to 25 ms on HDD, 0.1 to 0.5 ms on a metadata class). A segment freed by CP N is reusable after CP N+1 commits, after N+2 on `flush-opaque` backstores (C07). A dirty boot CRC-checks every summary page of the selected CP before export (about 8 s for a 4 GiB HDD CP, under 1 s on flash) and rewinds one CP on failure, so a controller that landed the superblock before the data is caught at boot rather than served as EIO.

**Map, stable block identity, holes, `diff`.** Per-volume radix map: a page index (16 B per 4 MiB: `page_pba`, `max_birth`) and 16 KiB pages of 1,024 entries `{addr: lseg:36 | off:16 | plen:12, birth: u32, crc32c: u32}`, 0.4% of volume size; every read verifies its CRC against the entry, which also catches misdirected writes. The CP log is the map's journal; an overlay answers lookups since the last checkpoint; a checkpoint rewrites dirty pages copy-on-write at 1 GiB of log or ten minutes, so boot replay stays under 5 s. Holes are entries with a birth (the OpenZFS hole-birth lesson). `addr` names a logical segment; the segment table maps `lseg` to `Physical(pseg)` or `Relocated(table)`, and each segment-table entry carries its `geom` (full stripe at birth) so a reshaped pool keeps the geometry of older segments (R19). GC moves a victim's live blocks into new stripes (birth preserved), updates the live root through the summary back-references, writes a relocation table (8 B per moved block), frees the physical segment under deferred reuse and never touches a pinned root; snapshot and clone entries still naming `lseg` resolve through the table at one cached extra lookup. Tables retire when every root predating the cleaning is gone; a weekly walk rewrites stale clone entries so tables older than 35 days retire. Without this, one snapshot-referenced live block would pin its stripe for the life of the schedule. `diff(base)` walks pages with `max_birth > base` and emits entries with `birth > base` plus holes.

**Geometry, caches, probe.** Trust order: md sysfs (`md/level`, `md/chunk_size`, `md/raid_disks`; stripe = chunk times data disks, `optimal_io_size` as a cross-check); block-queue sysfs and the `BLK*` ioctls; `vpd_pgb0` (optional, read to EOF, validated before parsing, `optimal_io_size = 0` defers to the probe, C35); the TOML override; then the probe, mandatory for non-md backstores and a check on md: candidates 64 KiB to 8 MiB by doubling, 16 GiB of S-sized O_DIRECT writes per candidate at random S-aligned offsets over a 16 GiB span at QD 8 (random placement defeats a write-back cache's coalescing); the full stripe is the smallest S within 10% of the best; a knee at `max_sectors_kb` means the controller does not coalesce split commands; a weekly 1 GiB re-probe raises CRIT on a knee shift. Fusion-class PERCs cap commands at 256 KiB (1 MiB with large-I/O firmware); MFI-series cards at 128 KiB (C23).

Write-back controllers report WCE=0 (C06), the kernel strips every flush and FUA (C05) and `megaraid_sas` never forwards a drive-cache flush (C07), so ordering rests on the attestation alone: `cache_protection = { controller: Bbu | FlashBacked | None, disk_cache: Disabled | Enabled | Unknown, learn_mode: transparent | write_through_fallback | unknown }`. `Enabled` and `Unknown` disk caches are refused for HDD VDs without a power-loss-protection attestation; `controller = none` yields `flush-opaque` handling; the attestation also runs on md members whose INQUIRY vendor names a RAID controller (C08). The attestation is shown as "attested, not verified" in `otapctl pool show` and in every alert. On a write-through controller (or a BBU fallback) full-stripe writes avoid read-modify-write only when the controller sees the stripe in one command, so strip times data disks is at most the measured `max_sectors_kb` and `inflight_stripes` follows the measured drain. The CP engine publishes `drain_rate_bytes_s` and raises WARN after three CPs below 50% of the probed rate, naming the three likely causes (learn cycle, patrol read, rebuild). Discard: `Discard::Never` by default; free segments are reused by whole-stripe overwrite; on flash behind such a controller `write_stop` at 90% is the only mitigation. A lone NVMe uses 1 MiB write units, 64 MiB segments and whole-segment `BLKDISCARD` after RZAT verification; NVMe advertises a write cache only when Identify Controller reports VWC.

**Replication, sender side.** Steady state: each staged ingest stripe of an outbound group goes to the flusher and the shipper at once; the shipper encrypts it into the transport's bounce buffer and the stripe's staging is released once the disk write has completed and the encrypted copy exists. Later resends re-read the stripe from cache or disk by `(lseg, stripe_idx)` through `DeltaSource::read_chunk`, routed by `MediaProfile.ship_readback` (default `cache`); the shipper never issues I/O to the HDD pool itself. Catch-up and resync use `Increment` mode: for each retained snapshot S_i in (base, T], walk S_i's pinned page index for `birth in (S_{i-1}, S_i]` in PBA order, apply as one receiver CP and materialize S_i; then the live root for (S_last, T]; capped at 30% of pool read bandwidth; the round ends when the live root's increment is one CP. Relationship state is per consistency group (default: the SVM's volumes, root included); the transport is per aggregate pair. Sender state machine: Idle to CatchUp at creation; Steady to Lagging when the hold is exceeded or the link is down over 10 s; Lagging to CatchUp when the link returns (pin T); CatchUp to Steady once the last increment is applied; any to Resync on a base mismatch; any to NoCommonBase when the pin crosses `break_at` or no common snapshot survives; NoCommonBase to Resync on a common scheduled snapshot. Both sides pin the newest common snapshot and T; `on_space_pressure = break | hold` breaks at `gc_urgent`; a day away at 50 MB/s of unique overwrite holds 4.3 TB on a 100 TB pool. After a failover the fenced old owner rolls back to the common snapshot, keeps `divergent-<ts>` for seven days and becomes the receiver.

**Replication, receiver side.** Chunks are containers of `(vol, lba, birth, data)` records staged through the receiver's own CP assembler into shadow roots; the receiver builds full stripes for its own geometry and commits through its own `Backstore`. At `ShipApply` every volume of the group switches roots together, so a replica is always at a sender CP boundary. The receiver forces a CP on `CpEnd` when none is in flight and runs a 2 s CP timer while a relationship is inbound; `applied_age_s` and `landed_age_s` are reported separately; the gate reads `applied_age_s`. Resume is a per-CP chunk bitmap plus one byte offset per open connection, keyed by `(cp_seq, cp_nonce)`; a `CpBegin` with a known sequence and a different nonce discards the partial. Applied lag is one CP interval plus CP bytes divided by min(link, receiver drain) plus the receiver commit: about 9 s at 1 GB/s on HDD, 14 s at 300 MB/s, 10 s on flash at 1 GB/s.

**nvfail.** Superblock v2 (four slots, each a full stripe) carries `run_state`, `write_active`, `acked_bytes`, `nvfail` and `nvfail_cause` per volume (`DirtyBoot | TornCp | Promote`), `rollback_gen` per volume, `freed_pending`, the map, segment, relocation, CP-log and snapshot roots, and per inbound group `applied_cp_seq`, `applied_owner_epoch`, `cp_age_at_send_ms`, `recv_mono` and `boot_id`. Superblock slots are for commits only; `write_active` lives in two intent slots with a monotonic `intent_seq`, and boot takes the union of the max-select superblock and any newer intent slot. A volume is active from its first write after idle until two consecutive CPs without a write; the idle-to-active transition writes an intent slot before the first acknowledgement (0.1 to 0.5 ms on a metadata class, up to 250 ms behind the stripe queue on HDD without one); `nvfail_intent = conservative` treats every writable online volume as active and is the v0.1 default. Boot with `run_state == Running`: verify the selected CP's summaries, union `write_active`, set `nvfail` for volumes whose class says so, write the superblock before any export, alert with the CP sequence, the window, per-volume `acked_bytes` and the clear command. Promotion: every writable volume of the group not provably idle (two consecutive `CpBegin`s with the bit clear and no later heartbeat write) takes its class posture. `rollback_gen` is bumped on every rollback and folded by `nbladed` into the export `fsid` and the LUN serial generation.

**Snapshots.** `Snapshot { vol, name, uuid, cp_seq, created_wall, kind: Fsfreeze | Crash, origin: Scheduled | User | Transfer | Divergent, pins }`: a pinned root plus a deadlist merged into the next older on deletion; schedules run in `dbladed` from the policy in Raft mirrored into the aggregate. NAS snapshots: `cut_within(3 s)`, `FIFREEZE` inside the SVM's mount namespace, the flip, thaw at once; past `freeze_timeout_ms` the snapshot is taken as `Crash`. Only `Fsfreeze` snapshots are mounted `ro,nouuid,norecovery`; a `Crash` snapshot is exposed only as a writable clone after a replay mount. Exposure: a read-only ublk device over the pinned root, each NFS-visible mount with its own export and a deterministic `fsid` from `(vol_uuid, snap_uuid)`, because every snapshot carries the live volume's XFS UUID and mountd resolves a UUID to the first match (C19); SMB Previous Versions through `vfs_shadow_copy2` with an absolute `shadow:snapdir`; clones get a replay mount and `xfs_admin -U generate`. Accounting per volume: `used`, `snapshot_used`, `replication_hold_bytes`, `unique(snap)`.

**Memory model** is the single table in 6.4.

### 1.4 Bottlenecks

Link versus media (10GbE moves 1.1 GB/s; an empty 12-drive RAID6 absorbs 1.2 to 2 GB/s and an aged one 0.3 to 0.5 GB/s; a 2 GB/s flash pool needs 25GbE for steady state). The 2 GiB shipper hold lasts 20 s at a 0.1 GB/s deficit and 2.3 s at 0.9 GB/s. Map traffic under random writes makes the flash metadata class mandatory above about 20 K sustained random IOPS on HDD and recommended above 15 TB; cold map lookups on HDD halve random-read IOPS outside the 6 GiB cache. A write-through controller that does not coalesce turns a 2.5 MiB stripe into ten read-modify-writes (150 to 300 MB/s). A controller whose BBU died under "write back regardless" loses its cache on power loss with no flush ever requested; the attestation and the torn-CP rule are the only defences. Hole-birth-class bugs ship unless `diff(base) == model(base)` is tested over random snapshot pairs with GC between them. A snapshot requested during a CP waits only for the flip now, but the NAS freeze still adds the page-cache sync (2 to 3 s worst case). A receiver reboot mid-CP re-sends up to 10 s of chunks. The divergent snapshot can pin one arena plus the unshipped CPs (60 GB at a 60 s lag and 1 GB/s). Boot after a crash is under 10 s at 50 TB plus the summary verification.

### 1.5 Creative solutions

The stripe image as the only chunk format, so the on-disk stripe, the CP log entry and the wire chunk are one structure and a chunk is re-derivable from disk by `(lseg, stripe_idx)` after any failure. Logical segments with relocation tables, so GC can clean around pinned snapshot roots without rewriting them and without pinning whole stripes. Holes with births. The generation ring that decouples the cut from the flush. The intent slot that keeps the nvfail list exact for one FUA page per burst. The random-placement probe that defeats a write-back cache. The `kind` bits and per-group packing that keep GC relocations and inbound applies off the wire. `dump --since <snap>` to a file as both the backup feature and the Phase B bridge.

### 1.6 Holes Storage found in the others

In Protocols: the Phase B block move was 8 to 15 s against a 3 s promise (fixed by standby block paths and a parallel ladder); one MAC on two nodes fights the switch while a wedged node is on the wire (fixed by the `arping -D` gate); BUILTIN and well-known SIDs get different ids in every SVM (fixed by seeding); a QoS-throttled NAS volume consumes the node-wide dirty pool (fixed by per-bdi caps and `UBLK_IO_F_META` exemption); the LUN unmap gives PDL only if every path reports it (the identity-change ruling); `ublks_max` is 64; rpcbind needs `-w`; regenerating `passdb.tdb` discards password changes; the iSCSI latency bar contradicted round 2. In Fabrics: the manifest was not stable across the arena-to-disk source flip and the per-extent CRC was not free (fixed by the stripe image); discards and volume events were missing from the delta; shipping from the arena held back HDD ingest (fixed by the retention policy and cache read-back); the receiver's apply is a full CP and the pending table was undersized; coalescing dropped snapshots (fixed by tags); a sender restart could not resume the same manifest (fixed by the nonce and the on-disk chunk); 1 MiB O_DIRECT landings onto a hardware VD were sub-stripe (fixed by landing through the engine's segment writer); cloning registered buffers needs a sparse source table. In Consensus: the manual window beyond the lag bound was longer than every client's hold (fixed by always-promote with posture); "Unavailable with PDL-style presentation" was a contradiction; a hardware RAID stall became a 5 s self-takeover (withdrawn; `backstore_stall_tolerance` 60 s); `cp_age_at_send` understated loss by one flush (age from `cut_mono`); the page bitset was too small and aggregate placement contradicted SVM mobility (per-SVM slots); pre-copy converged on link rate when the floor is landing rate; file class `off` let NFSv4.1 clients write holes into rolled-back files (`fence`); `ProtectHostname=` cannot name an SVM.

### 1.7 What the review changed

Storage accepted every hole raised against it: the CP nonce; hold release on disk completion plus the encrypted copy, with resends from disk; the generation ring and `cut_within`; the per-LUN alignment detector for `Esxi512e` on HDD; the corrected ublk path; the attestation struct with disk-cache and learn-mode fields and its extension to md members; the random probe from 64 KiB and the weekly re-probe; the receiver `ShipApply` trigger and the separate applied and landed ages; dirty-boot summary verification; relocations kept off the wire by `kind` bits and per-group packing; the conservative promotion set; superblock slots for commits only; full-stripe metadata and intent slots on parity pools; the intent slot on the metadata class; explicit per-snapshot exports; the root-volume content check; consistency groups as the unit of relationship state; ordered snapshot increments for catch-up; per-device-class flush ordering; the stream fields the gate needs. It rejected two things, both confirmed by the fact checks: the claimed double flush of `RWF_DSYNC` on the io_uring path (block/fops.c syncs only inline completions) and raising the throttle threshold under a controller's write-through window (R7's 2 s cap stands).

### 1.8 Revised Phase C recommendation

Eleven work items with exit tests (record, Storage round 3, section 4), folded into the 12-week plan of Part 8: format v2 frozen; arena and re-attach; dirty index and ring; geometry and attestation; CP engine with the commit protocol and dm-log-writes proof; map with `diff`; nvfail in conservative mode; a simple cleaner with reserves; snapshots as pinned roots with clone LUNs; the ublk front door; `DeltaSource` in both modes with `dump --since` and `restore`. Phase B adds the TCP `DeltaSink`, `ShipApply`, nonce-keyed resume, per-group relationship state, the conservative promotion set, resync in both directions, `on_space_pressure`, the 2 GiB hold and receiver staging. Phase A adds aggregate import and export on shared disks, the epoch-page check at CP submit and superblock completion, and the priced `ShelfSsdRing`.

### 1.9 Risks Storage carries forward

## Part 2. Opus-Protocols (SVMs, NAS, block targets, the client's view of nvfail)

### 2.1 Executive take

With no synchronous mirror, every protocol's acknowledgement latency collapses to the engine's local figure and FLUSH and COMMIT stay ordering barriers; nothing in the protocol stack waits for a partner. The SVM stops being a migration mechanism and becomes the unit of namespaces, configuration, identity, replication consistency and mobility. Single-node-first means SVM create, start, stop and LIF move must be perfect on one node before any cross-node move exists; a Phase B move is then "stop here, ship, start there" with the same ladder, and an unplanned failover is that ladder plus a rollback, which turns nvfail into a per-volume, protocol-visible state for NAS as well as for LUNs. Kernel 6.12 lets block protocols live inside the SVM network namespace, so iSCSI and NVMe/TCP are SVM protocols with SVM LIFs, exactly as in ONTAP; only Fibre Channel stays at node level, with NPIV vports per SVM where a fabric exists.

### 2.2 Libraries and kernel interfaces

### 2.3 Architecture

**Namespaces, pins, units.** An SVM is three namespaces, network, UTS and mount, pinned under `/run/opentap/svm/<id>/ns/{net,uts,mnt}` on a private bind of `/run/opentap`. `nbladed` spawns `unshare --net=P/net --uts=P/uts --mount=P/mnt --propagation slave --fork -- otap-nsinit <id>`: unshare's child stays in the host mount namespace and binds `/proc/<ppid>/ns/*` once the parent's namespace changes, because a process cannot bind its own mount-namespace file (C16). `otap-nsinit` sets the nodename, brings `lo` up, builds the view, pivots and exits; the pins hold the namespaces with no holder process, about 5 ms and 2 MiB per SVM. Units are transient under `svm-<id>.slice` with `ExecStart=/usr/libexec/otap/otap-nsexec <id> <profile> -- <daemon>`: the wrapper enters the pins, drops the bounding and permitted capability sets to the daemon's profile, sets `PR_SET_NO_NEW_PRIVS`, applies a per-daemon seccomp allow-list and execs. Units keep the cgroup and seccomp-only options (`DevicePolicy=closed` with `DeviceAllow`, `RestrictAddressFamilies`, `LockPersonality`, `RestrictRealtime`, `RestrictSUIDSGID`, `NoNewPrivileges`, `KillMode=control-group`) and never `ProtectHostname`, `RestrictNamespaces`, `SystemCallFilter` or a mount-based option, which would stop the `setns` (C15, C16). Not PID, user, IPC, cgroup or time namespaces. In v0.1 tenant daemons run as root inside the view with the wrapper's capability drop as a stretch item, and the documentation says so.

**The view.** `/` is a private tmpfs; the host's `/usr /bin /sbin /lib /lib64` are bound read-only; `/etc` files are overlaid from `/run/opentap/svm/<id>/etc`, rendered from Raft at every start; `/run` is a private tmpfs with the journal and notify sockets bound in; `/var/lib/nfs` is a tmpfs with the root volume's state directories bound; the per-netns `nfsd` filesystem is mounted at `/proc/fs/nfsd` from inside the netns; `/vol/<svm>_root` is a recursive slave bind of the host's root-volume mount, and each top-level junction is one bind to `/`. `/dev` is masked to seven nodes, there is no `/sys`, `/proc/sys` is read-only, and only the SVM's own `/run/opentap/svm/<id>` is visible. Propagation does the rest: a volume mounted later on the host under `/vol/<svm>_root` appears in the view in the same syscall. The host's `/vol` is a tmpfs so mount-point directories never land on the boot disk. Top-level junction names are reserved against the system directories and match `[a-z0-9_-]{1,64}`.

**Junctions, and the mount-point hole.** A junction `/vm/iso` is a volume mounted by `nbladed` in the host namespace at `/vol/<svm>_root/vm/iso`. The ONTAP veteran's finding stands as the most dangerous hole in the SVM design: when a child is unmounted (nvfail, a failed mount, the stop ladder) the directory remains, empty, writable and still exported with its explicit `fsid`, so an ESXi host under `no_root_squash` writes `.lck` files and vmdks into the 1 GiB root volume and masks the outage. The rule (R51): mount-point directories are root-owned, mode 000, with the immutable attribute; an export is added only after the mount is verified and removed before the unmount; children are unmounted with their parent; `otapctl volume show` prints the parenthesised junction state; a nvfailed volume answers ESTALE and a planned move hangs clients so they retry.

**Root volume versus Raft.** The root volume (1 GiB thin, typically under 100 MiB used) holds everything a daemon mutates by itself: `secrets.tdb` and `schannel_store.tdb`, the keytab, `winbindd_idmap.tdb` and the other persistent tdbs, statd's `sm` directories and state number, nfsdcld's SQLite, and `passdb.tdb`, which is authoritative (Raft holds the declared user list and the initial sealed hash; `otapctl user` writes through `pdbedit`; regeneration only when the tdb is absent). Raft holds everything an operator declares and `nbladed` renders it into tmpfs at every start. The idmap seed is rendered from a Raft table of well-known and BUILTIN SIDs and applied with `net idmap restore`, `net groupmap add` and `net sam addmem` before the first winbindd start and on every rehost, because `net idmap set mapping` is a stub in 4.22 (C33). The root volume is in the SVM's consistency group; a `secrets.tdb` change (watched with inotify) requests an early aggregate CP, because an out-of-band CP of one volume does not exist. Every successful start records the root's `(cp_seq, rollback_gen)`; a later mismatch raises WARN "root rewound" with the machine-account and NFSv4-reclaim consequences.

**knfsd per netns.** `nfsdctl autostart` inside the view reads the SVM's `nfs.conf` (`scope`, `threads`, `grace-time=20`, `lease-time=10`, versions, listeners) and sends `THREADS_SET` with `NFSD_A_SERVER_SCOPE`. Because `nfsd_svc()` rewrites the scope from the caller's UTS nodename on every scope-less thread write and refuses a scope on a running server (C14), the UTS nodename equals the scope string and every later thread change is issued from inside that UTS namespace; `nfsdctl threads` printing `scope:` is the post-condition (R34). `nfsdcld -s /vol/<svm>_root/.otap/nfs/nfsdcld -p /var/lib/nfs/rpc_pipefs` runs before nfsd on every NFS SVM, with readiness defined as the nfsdcld PID holding the `cld` pipe open, because `nfsd4_cld_tracking_init` waits only one second and then silently refuses every reclaim; an empty reclaim table skips the v4 grace. `rpc.statd -F -L -P <root dir> -n <svm fqdn> -p 32765 -o 32766`; after a start on a new node `sm-notify -f -P <root dir> -v <svm fqdn>`, the FQDN resolving through `/etc/hosts` to a current LIF. `rpcbind -f -w` on the private `/run`. Early grace end: read `v4_end_grace`, write `Y` once `/proc/fs/nfsd/clients` shows every nfsdcld client reclaimed, poll until it reads `Y`, because Debian's 6.12.69 and later schedule the end and return `-EBUSY` in more cases than the tag. One global `lockd` thread serves every SVM's NLM, so a cold-inode open on a stalled volume blocks every SVM's `NLM_LOCK` for the stall; Proxmox datastores are recommended as NFSv4.1 and a stalled volume is unexported before any other ladder step. Per-NAS-volume dirty pool: `/sys/class/bdi/<dev>/max_bytes` equals the QoS bucket's drain rate times `dirty_expire` (floor 64 MiB) with `strict_limit=1`; XFS log writes carry `REQ_META`, which ublk passes as `UBLK_IO_F_META`, so COMMIT latency is never held by the bucket. Templates: a datastore SVM is NFSv3 only with 32 threads; a file SVM is NFSv4.1 only with lease 10 s, grace 20 s and delegations off. The NLM hole shared with ONTAP: a client that recorded a LIF address the SVM no longer owns is never notified; `nbladed` alerts on `sm` entries whose `my_name` is not a current LIF.

**Samba 4.22.** One smbd (and one winbindd when AD-joined) per SVM, never nmbd; `netbios name` is the 15-character SVM name; `private dir`, `state directory` and `cache directory` on the root volume; `lock directory`, `pid directory`, `ncalrpc dir` and `winbindd socket directory` in the private `/run`; `kernel oplocks = no` and `kernel share modes = no`; `server min protocol = SMB3_00`, `server signing = mandatory`, `smb encrypt = desired`; `idmap config * : backend = tdb` with the seeded range disjoint from every domain range, `rid` per domain with a Raft base, never `autorid`; trusted domains enumerated at join. Each SVM joins AD as its own computer object with its own SPNs, machine password and keytab; `svm delete` runs `net ads leave`. Durable handles live in `smbXsrv_open_global.tdb` under the lock directory with `TDB_CLEAR_IF_FIRST` and do not survive an smbd restart (C34): every planned move and `svm stop/start` is documented as handle loss; a handle with a lease and no byte-range lock survives a LIF blip shorter than the negotiated timeout. The twelve identity collisions and their rules are in the record (Protocols round 1, section 2.6): unique 15-character names, `net ads leave`, per-SVM `krb5.conf`, the winbind socket in the private `/run`, `rid` with Raft bases, local Unix accounts in 1000000 to 1999999, unmapped NFS `sec=sys` uids, S3 objects as the gateway uid, machine-password rotation versus lag, Windows-set share permissions in `share_info.tdb` flagged for drift, ports per namespace, per-SVM DNS registration.

**versitygw.** One per SVM inside the namespaces, `--versioning-dir` on the same volume as the gateway root, IAM rendered from Raft, TLS per SVM, `GOMEMLIMIT` at 80% of the slice, the admin API not exposed. `--object-lock-mode` is publish locking for conditional writes, not S3 Object Lock, and the fix that stores bucket default retention on each object (PR #2479) merged on 2026-10-08 into no tagged release (C40); the shipped binary is pinned to the first release containing it and the product page keeps the "experimental versioning" label. S3 leaves v0.1 (R46).

**Block targets.** One target per SVM, instantiated on both nodes of a pair, in the SVM's netns; each node has its own block LIFs and its own ALUA target port group (`tg_pt_gp_id` fixed per node and written once, identical on both nodes, `alua_access_type=1`, `implicit_trans_secs=10`, `preferred` on the home node). Vendor `LIO-ORG`, product `OpenTAP` and `vpd_unit_serial=<volume uuid>` are written before the first LUN link (C18); the built-in multipath entry for `LIO-ORG` is why the vendor string is permanent (C17). Per TPG `demo_mode_discovery=0` and `generate_node_acls=0`, so SendTargets from a tenant lists only its own target; `emulate_tpu=1`, `emulate_tpws=1`; never `emulate_write_cache`, which returns `EINVAL` on iblock (C09): `dbladed` sets `UBLK_ATTR_VOLATILE_CACHE | UBLK_ATTR_FUA` before `START_DEV` and the reconciler asserts `/sys/block/ublkbN/queue/write_cache == "write back"` and `queue/fua == 1` before linking any LUN. Data LUNs are mapped at 1 to 255 and the Raft validator refuses a data LUN at mapped LUN 0 per (TPG, ACL), because LIO's virtual LUN 0 answers an unmapped LUN 0 with DATA PROTECT and a read-only dummy, never with the PDL sense (C02). Portals are cluster-unique `ip:port`, never wildcard, IPv4 only in Phase C, created inside the netns and checked there with `ss -ltn`; teardown removes sessions and portals, waits for `np_exports` to reach zero, then deletes the LIF devices, then the pins, because a leftover socket pins a dead SVM's namespace on the wire. Client-visible LUNs present `size` and grow by an ALUA-rolled re-create (standby first, group flip, then the other side; a planned 3 to 5 s pause on a single node), because 6.12 has no `UPDATE_SIZE` and both paths must report the same capacity (C10). `options ublk_drv ublks_max=4096` in the platform profile; the device budget counts own LUNs, standby replicas and snapshot clones.

**LIFs.** NAS LIFs are macvlan in bridge mode on the data port (single port, active-backup bond or LACP bond, in that order of recommendation) with a persistent MAC `02:4f:54:` plus three bytes of `hash(cluster, lif)`, created with `IFLA_NET_NS_FD` straight into the netns and announced by `nbladed`'s own gratuitous ARP at 0, 300 ms and 1 s (the kernel never announces an added address, C36; IPv6 adds `ndisc_notify=1` and three unsolicited NAs). On an unplanned failover the survivor raises a NAS LIF only after `arping -D -c 3 -w 1 -I <parent> <addr>` returns 0 (no reply in DAD mode), with the watchdog deadline as the backstop, so a wedged node delays the survivor by at most 8 s instead of fighting it. Before a NAS address is removed, `nbladed` runs `ss -K` inside the netns on every established connection to it, so clients reconnect on their first probe after the LIF returns rather than after a TCP back-off; whether `ss -K` reaches sunrpc's kernel-owned sockets is [believed], with an `AF_PACKET` RST per connection as the fallback. Block LIFs never move. Per-SVM nftables rulesets are stateless (accept by LIF address and service port, drop the rest, no conntrack). The `LifSpec` gains ONTAP's fields (R52): home node, home port, failover group as a set of `(node, port)` in one L2 domain, failover policy `LocalOnly | FollowSvm | Disabled`, `auto_revert`, and a `lif revert` verb; under a bond, a port is the bond.

**Resource governance and QoS.** Slices govern userspace only: smbd, winbindd, versitygw and the NFS helper daemons, plus the page cache they write. nfsd and lockd kernel threads are node-wide and unattributed; the root cgroup outweighs any slice. The per-volume token bucket (`QosPolicyGroup`, R53) runs in the ublk handler at about 20 ns per request: 1 ms refill, delay never fail, a 1 s hold cap, exempt from the 2 s stall detector, re-checking the epoch at release, `REQ_META` bypass; NAS volumes add the per-bdi caps. Template defaults: datastore `CPUWeight=200`, file 100, backup 50; `MemoryMax` from the memory table.

**nvfail from the client's side.** The table is in 6.6, together with the posture vocabulary.

**Rollout.** The client matrix per protocol step (record, Protocols round 1, section 2.12) stands as the qualification programme for v0.2; v0.1 is qualified against Debian 13 initiators and clients in CI plus one ESXi host if the owner provides one (Part 8).

### 2.4 Bottlenecks

Portal address collision across IPspaces (two SVMs declaring one `ip:port` share one socket silently; the Raft validator and a nightly `ss -ltn` check). Lingering namespaces held by sockets. XFS log replay at an unplanned start (20 volumes with 128 MiB logs: 5 s in parallel at array speed, 50 s when each read costs a cold map read first). nfsd threads to zero wait for held requests. Grace on every start of a file SVM unless early end fires, and one absent client defeats early end. NLM clients not notified. Machine-password rotation inside the lost window (about 1 in 20,000 per rotation; the inotify-triggered CP closes it to the CP latency). macvlan CPU at 1,500-byte frames (0.25 core per 10GbE port at line rate, 0.04 at 9,000; MTU 9000 is recommended with a path-MTU check, never a default). The root cgroup outweighs slices. smbd per-connection memory under `MemoryMax`. versitygw's Object Lock gaps. 64 SVMs are about 2,000 private mounts, 200 pins, 64 nfsd instances and 64 winbindds polling a DC; a homelab needs two to eight.

### 2.5 Creative solutions

The `pivot_root`ed view that makes every protocol see ONTAP paths while the host sees `/vol/<svm>_root/...`. Daemon-owned state on the root volume and declared state regenerated from Raft, so there is never a stale copy to reconcile. The per-netns block target, so iSCSI identity, CHAP, ACLs and discovery are tenant-scoped. The `arping -D` gate, which turns a wedged node's wire presence into an 8 s delay instead of a split identity. `ss -K` before address removal. The identity seed for well-known SIDs. The per-bdi dirty cap with metadata exemption, which makes a throttled tenant block alone.

## Part 3. Opus-Fabrics (the asynchronous stream, Fibre Channel)

### 3.1 Executive take

The pivot deletes the hardest half of the round-2 fabrics design and makes the other half ordinary. There is no put-to-Placed latency to chase, no inbound ring that must outlive a daemon, no per-record acknowledgement, no RDMA leg, no busy-polled mirror core and no reason for `otap-ringd` to exist. What replaces it is a bulk mover: every 5 to 15 s a CP produces a delta of hundreds of megabytes to a few gigabytes, and the transport's job is to get it onto the partner's disk at line rate, resume it from any chunk after any failure, prove it arrived intact and cost as little CPU per byte as possible. That is a TCP problem. Fibre Channel changes role too: as a replication transport it would move at most 1.45 GB/s (16G) through a kernel target, a staging file and a scanner, for a cost a 25GbE DAC beats at the price of two used NICs; as a client fabric it is what the round-2 facts say it is, in-box and buildable, now as the first step of Phase B.

### 3.2 Libraries and kernel interfaces

Crates (C39): io-uring 0.7.15, rustls 0.23.45 with aws-lc-rs (the unbuffered API: `WriteTraffic::encrypt` writes TLS records straight into a registered bounce buffer), crc-fast 1.10.0 (crc32c 0.6.8 dropped), blake3 1.8.7, lz4_flex 0.14.0 and zstd 0.14.2 (not in v0.1), zerocopy 0.8.62, rustix 1.1.5, postcard 1.1.3, memmap2 0.9.11, tokio 1.53.2 for the control plane only. Kernel: `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC` (47) with `IORING_RECVSEND_FIXED_BUF` and `IORING_SEND_ZC_REPORT_USAGE`, the two-CQE model (`CQE_F_MORE`, then `CQE_F_NOTIF` when the buffer may be reused, `NOTIF_USAGE_ZC_COPIED` when the NIC path copied), `IORING_REGISTER_CLONE_BUFFERS` (30, whole-table clone into an empty ring only), `IORING_OP_RECV`; no `READV_FIXED`/`WRITEV_FIXED` (6.15), no `RECV_ZC` (6.15); `SENDMSG_ZC` does not accept fixed buffers on 6.12 (C24); kTLS refuses `SEND_ZC` with `-EOPNOTSUPP` because `__tcp_set_ulp` clears `SOCK_SUPPORT_ZC` (C26, the one refuted claim: the conclusion held, the mechanism did not). qla2xxx: `qlini_mode` is load-time only with `exclusive | disabled | enabled | dual` and a misspelling fails `qlt_init` (C27); `tcm_qla2xxx` registers the `qla2xxx` fabric (only `tpgt_1`) and the `qla2xxx_npiv` fabric (lport named `<phys wwpn>@<16 hex>:<16 hex>`), NPIV requires an F_Port fabric login and `max_npiv_vports` defaults to 63 (C28); no NVMe-FC target role exists, only the initiator (C29). ESXi: `Disk.MaxLUN` is an exclusive bound over 0 to 16,383, the default PSP for `VMW_SATP_ALUA` is `VMW_PSP_MRU`, and qlnativefc below 5.4.80 cannot log into direct-attached targets on 8.0.x (C30). Proxmox VE 9.1 (Debian 13.2, kernel 6.17.2) carries multipath-tools 0.11.1-2 with the built-in `(LIO-ORG|SUSE)` entry: `GROUP_BY_PRIO`, `prio alua`, `path_checker directio`, `no_path_retry 12`, immediate failback (C17, C31).

### 3.3 Architecture

**The pipe, the delta, the chunk.** A pipe is a TLS session set between two aggregates (one per direction per pair). A delta is `(rel, base, target, cp_id)`: steady state ships one committed CP's ingest stripes in staging order; catch-up, resync and coalesced deltas are map walks in PBA order per consistency group, chunked as the live blocks of each on-disk stripe born after `base`, with the delta's segment list GC-pinned from `describe()` to `DeltaEnd`. A chunk is one stripe image, at most 8 MiB (2.5 MiB on a 12-drive HDD RAID6, 256 KiB on flash), opaque to the transport except for its CRC.

**Framing.** A 64-byte little-endian header laid out with zerocopy: `magic u32, ver u16, kind u8, flags u8 (codec) | rel u32, idx u32 | cp_seq u64 | base u64 | cp_id u64 | owner_epoch u64 | wire_len u32, raw_len u32 | payload_crc u32, hdr_crc u32`. Kinds: `Hello, HelloAck, DeltaBegin, VolEvent, Chunk, Holes, DeltaEnd, Landed, Committed, Abort, Ping, Pong`. `Hello` carries node id, pipe id, direction epoch and capabilities and is refused when the epoch is stale; `HelloAck` the cursor; `DeltaBegin` chunk count, bytes, snapshot tags and `cut_age_ms` so both ages are measured from `cut_mono`; `VolEvent` (create with geometry and class, resize, delete, `SnapTag{uuid, name, retention}`) and `Holes` (16 B entries) are ordered inside the delta before any chunk that references the volume; `Abort(UnknownVol)` is retried every second for 60 s while the receiver's Raft follower applies `VolState`, then the relationship is `NoCommonBase` with that reason; `DeltaEnd` carries a BLAKE3 digest over the summary pages and is sent only after the sender's superblock write completed. Golden vectors and property tests freeze the format first; the parser is safe Rust, fuzzed.

**Cursor, credits, integrity.** The receiver owns the cursor (`applied`, `applied_epoch`, `landing: (cp_seq, cp_id)`, `landed_contig`, `landed_sparse`, `credits`), persisted in a 4 KiB page per pipe at every receiver CP; landing is idempotent by `(cp_id, idx)`; a sender restart resumes at `landed_contig`; a different `cp_id` for the same `cp_seq` discards the partial; a receiver restart re-lands whatever its last CP did not record. Credits are 4 KiB units of free receiver staging granted in `Landed` frames as the receiver's segment writer stages stripes, initial grant 64 MiB, capped at the staging size (2 GiB), so the sender is bounded by the landing rate; TCP windows (16 MiB maxima, autotuned) are the second layer. Integrity: per-4 KiB CRC in the summary page, CRC32C per chunk, BLAKE3 per delta; a bad header CRC drops the connection; three `Abort(Corrupt, idx)` for one chunk make the sender re-read the stripe from disk bypassing cache and verify every 4 KiB CRC; cache-only corruption resends and counts `cache_corruption`, an on-disk mismatch makes the relationship `NoCommonBase(MediaError{pba})` with a media alert; a chunk is never skipped.

**TLS and zero copy.** TLS 1.3 with mutual authentication against the cluster CA on every endpoint; `tls = false` and `direct = true` are deleted (an unauthenticated `Hello` would let any host on a recabled "direct" link write attacker-chosen bytes into the replica at a valid CP boundary). rustls encrypts the staged stripe into a registered bounce buffer (two 8 MiB halves per connection, 4 KiB pages, so the huge-page truesize concern of C25 cannot apply); the half goes out as one `IORING_OP_SEND_ZC` with `IORING_RECVSEND_FIXED_BUF`, `MSG_WAITALL` and `IORING_SEND_ZC_REPORT_USAGE`, and is reused at the `NOTIF` completion; a peer reporting `ZC_COPIED` on 500 of 1,000 sends (tap ports, no-scatter-gather devices, tcpdump) falls back to `IORING_OP_SEND`. The arena is never a send source and never registered for sends. Receiver: `IORING_OP_RECV` into an 8 MiB buffer, in-place decrypt, CRC, then `land()`, whose copy into the engine's staging is the third pass. Compression `auto`: zstd level 3 on two workers at 1GbE, LZ4 behind a sampled ratio gate at 10GbE, off at 25GbE and above; one pass into an 8 MiB scratch before encryption. Cost: 0.4 to 0.5 core per side at 10GbE for TLS [believed].

**Connections, endpoints, throughput.** `connections = auto`: one at 10GbE and below, four at 25GbE and above; chunks round-robin; control frames on connection 0; a dead connection re-queues its unlanded chunks. Endpoints in priority order (DAC, then LAN): connect budget 1 s including TLS and `Hello`, dead after 3 s of silence with data pending, `Ping` every second when idle, two reconnects then the next endpoint, back-off 1 to 30 s, fail-back after 30 s of good probes; active/standby, never striping. A per-pipe, per-endpoint `throttle_kbps` (R62) caps the stream when it falls back to a shared LAN. Targets with TLS on at 1,500-byte MTU: 1GbE 110 MB/s on 0.2 core; 10GbE 1.0 GB/s on one connection (sender 0.8, receiver 1.3 cores); 25GbE 2.0 GB/s on four connections (sender 2, receiver 3 to 4 cores); 40 and 100GbE 3 GB/s, not promised, needing `RECV_ZC`. HDD destinations cap the pair at 0.5 GB/s empty and 0.3 GB/s aged regardless of link. Lag: `applied_age = CP interval + max(delta / link, delta / land rate) + receiver CP`; at 300 MB/s ingest, 10 s CPs, 10GbE and an HDD receiver landing 0.5 GB/s the age oscillates between 8 and 19 s and an unplanned failover rolls back at most 19 s plus the 3 s lease. Coalescing after two queued CPs bounds the backlog by the changed set and never crosses a snapshot tag.

**State tables.** The sender session (Idle, Connecting, Describing, Streaming, Ending, NoCommonBase) and the receiver (Listening, Sessioned, Landing) are in the record (Fabrics round 3, section 3.1) and are adopted as written; the endpoint selector (Preferred, Secondary, Down with the 3 s, 30 s and 1 to 30 s rows) is unchanged from round 1.

**FC as a transport.** Not built in Phases C, B or A. The only 6.12 mechanism is a landing LUN exported through `tcm_qla2xxx` on a tmpfs `fileio` or spare-NVMe `iblock` backstore, written by the sender's HBA in initiator mode at `idx x chunk` offsets, scanned by the receiver, with the control frames on the mTLS TCP link because FC carries no IP; FC-NVMe is impossible because qla2xxx has no target template (C29); the port arrangement needs `qlini_mode=dual` and per-port mode writes before any TPG is enabled, and a mode change is an ISP abort. Cost 8 to 10 engineer-weeks and a four-HBA lab for at most 0.7, 1.45 or 2.9 GB/s, each matched by a 10GbE or 25GbE DAC at the price of two used NICs. The envelope is transport-blind (chunks addressed by `(cp_id, idx)`), so an FC landing ring could follow v0.3 if an FC-only interconnect ever appears; the `FcLink` capability bits are dropped.

**FC client targets.** Module profile `options qla2xxx qlini_mode=disabled ql2xnvmeenable=0 ql2xmqsupport=1`, `ql2xfwloadbin=1` per the ISP25xx rule, `ql2xexchoffld=4096` on 16G and 32G cards; `qlini_mode` is load-time only and the installer validates the spelling (C27). The physical TPG is enabled once at boot (one ISP abort per port, 2 to 5 s) before any NPIV TPG and never disabled while vports serve, because enabling it logs out every vport. NPIV FC LIFs exist only behind an F_Port fabric login (C28); direct attach and loop use the physical TPG with one SVM per port (`shared_port = true` is an opt-in with node-wide LUN IDs and no tenant guarantee); at most 32 vports per port; `vport_state` is the LIF's operational state; vports do not move, each SVM gets one per node and ALUA decides the live path. WWNN per SVM `2A:SS:<OUI+serial of the home node's lowest WWPN>`, LIF WWPNs `2B:LL:<same six bytes>`, stored in Raft at creation; physical QLogic ports use `20:00`, `21:00` and `21:01`, so nothing collides [believed]. ALUA: implicit-only, support bits for Active/Optimized, Standby, Unavailable and Transitioning, never Active/Non-optimized, `implicit_trans_secs=10`, `preferred=1` on the home node; TPGS advertised from day one so nothing on any client changes when the second node arrives. The configfs tree, shared with `blade_lio`, is in the record (Fabrics round 3, section 3.3): `wwn` before any link, `attrib` without `emulate_write_cache`, both ALUA groups, LUNs 1 to 255, per-initiator ACLs, `enable` last; the installer creates `/etc/target` with `pr/` and `alua/tpgs_<group>/` before `target_core_mod` loads so `dbroot` never needs a write; `rtslib-fb-targetctl.service` is never installed and is masked if an operator adds it. ESXi recipe: `esxcli storage nmp satp rule add -s VMW_SATP_ALUA -V LIO-ORG -M OpenTAP -P VMW_PSP_RR -O iops=1 -c tpgs_on -e OpenTAP`, `Disk.PathEvalTime=30`, guest disk timeouts 180 s, `Disk.MaxLUN` untouched, qlnativefc 5.4.80 or later for direct attach (C30). Proxmox recipe: empty `multipath.conf` plus `find_multipaths "strict"`, `multipath -a`, `multipath-tools-boot`, PVE 9.1 volume-chain snapshots with the C31 preconditions (enabled before any disk exists, machine version 10 or later, `emulate_tpu`/`emulate_tpws`). Firmware facts per family (ISP24xx tolerated; ISP25xx `ql2500_fw.bin` 8.07.00 from `firmware-qlogic`, `ql2xfwloadbin=1` when flash is newer; ISP83xx, ISP2261 and ISP27xx flash-first) stand from round 2; `otapctl hba show` warns below the floors; firmware updates are the operator's job with Marvell's tools. Whether the inbox ESXi 8.0 qlnativefc still binds an ISP2532 (QLE2562) is unverified; the ESXi half of the FC tests uses a 16G QLE2692.

**Process topology.** `otap-ringd` is retired; the arena is a hugetlbfs file created by `run-opentap-arena.mount` and `opentap-arena-setup.service`; the shipper and the receiver are reactors inside `dbladed` (`catch_unwind` per reactor, mTLS peers only, fuzzed parser), on the non-isolated cores at a `CPUWeight` below the ublk reactors, never busy-polling; one such thread sustains 10GbE with TLS, four sustain 25GbE. `nbladed` owns configfs, sysfs, modprobe.d, netns, systemd units and the target trees and never touches the data path; `otap-ctl` owns the state store, leases, the slot page, the fence executor and the watchdog pet and never holds a data-path descriptor.

### 3.4 Bottlenecks

The receiver copy at 25GbE (1.2 to 2 GB/s per core [believed]; four connections on three cores reach 2 GB/s; 40GbE and up wait for `RECV_ZC`). A destination landing below the link (an aged RAID6 at 0.3 to 0.5 GB/s; credits throttle the sender, lag grows, coalescing bounds the backlog; the dashboard shows ship rate and land rate). Silent copies on tap ports, bridges and NICs without scatter-gather (the `ZC_COPIED` fallback). TLS and compression CPU. The physical TPG enable and `qlini_mode` changes reset the port (boot only). Exchange exhaustion at 1,024 outstanding commands (16 ESXi hosts at QD 64; `ql2xexchoffld=4096`). NPIV limits (firmware default 63 vports; Brocade 126 and Cisco 256 logins per F_Port [believed]; capped at 32). `Disk.MaxLUN` lowered by an operator hides LUNs above it. The ESXi direct-connect regression. Proxmox's 60 s queue under the built-in entry. The replica's LUN size on the standby without `UPDATE_SIZE` (re-create while Standby). Single-path direct-attach hosts have no transparent failover (documented, not solved). A delta larger than the receiver's free space minus the GC reserve is refused at `DeltaBegin`. Divergent CPs after a failover are refused by the base check and discarded by a `Rebase`. Intra-node loopback replication costs one core per GB/s and contends for one HBA.

### 3.5 Creative solutions

The transport-blind envelope with the engine's stripe image as the opaque payload, so the sender can resume from disk bytes identical to what already landed and a future FC or S3 sink needs no format change. The CP nonce in every frame. Credits as free receiver staging, which makes the landing rate the only back-pressure. Deterministic WWNN and WWPN allocation per SVM and LIF from the home node's serial, stored in Raft once. The ALUA policy that advertises TPGS from the first single-node LUN so the second node changes nothing on any client. The empty `multipath.conf` that rides the built-in `LIO-ORG` entry on every Debian and Proxmox host.

### 3.6 Holes Fabrics found in the others

In Storage: `CpEnd` could reach the receiver before the sender's superblock committed (the nonce and the commit-marker-after-commit rule); the staging hold was released by two different events; the catch-up walk shipped only the newest state, so every snapshot taken during lag was missing on the replica (ordered increments); a `Snapshot` trigger waited behind an in-flight CP (`cut_within` and the ring); applied lag was 10 to 29 s on HDD, not 10 to 19 (the forced receiver CP); the torn-CP rule only rewound when the map roots failed to verify (dirty-boot summary verification); the sequential probe could not find the stripe behind a write-back cache (random placement); the drain curve is suspended for hours by learn cycles, patrol reads and rebuilds (`learn_mode`, `drain_rate_bytes_s`, derived `inflight_stripes`); the attestation said nothing about the drives' own caches (`disk_cache`); "one flush, then the superblock" was wrong with a metadata class on a second device (per-class flush ordering); the stream records lacked the gate's fields and a single cursor could not describe four connections (the bitmap cursor); TLS and compression were priced as free on top of `SEND_ZC`; the intent write on a VD in write-through cost a full RMW (the metadata-class intent slot, conservative default without one). In Protocols: unmapping LUN 0 does not produce PDL (C02); SendTargets disclosed every SVM; the `*` idmap range was per-SVM; tenant daemons ran as unconfined root with the host's `/dev`, `/sys` and `/proc` in view (the wrapper and the masked view); the `multipath.conf` contradiction; Phase B block moves were LIF moves (standby paths); `fail fast and retry` omitted the TCP back-off and SMB handle loss (`ss -K`, documented loss); the NFS fence assumed ESXi stayed stuck on ESTALE (server-side withholding, C20); three memory numbers and three QD1 numbers for the same thing. In Consensus: `recv_mono` was garbage after a replica reboot (the `boot_id` guard, a blocker); the no-quorum `stop` argument ignored the guests inside the hypervisors; portals node-level versus per-SVM; the warm-standby mechanism; `base` in Raft wore the witness or lied (removed); nvfail defaults disagreed and the root had no class; P5 assumed commit on `CpEnd` (`commit: Immediate`); the gate's inputs were missing and two lag bounds existed; the arena owner process.

### 3.7 What the review changed

Fabrics accepted every hole raised against it. One change absorbed most of them: the chunk is the engine's stripe image and the transport owns only the envelope, the cursor, the credits and the connections; the manifest, `ExtentSrc`, `crc32c_combine` and the arena-as-send-source are deleted. TLS became mandatory, which deleted the zero-copy-from-the-arena path and three kernel-version hazards with it. `VolEvent` and `Holes` frames entered the delta; coalescing stopped crossing snapshot tags; `land()` hands blocks to the engine's segment writer; the receiver's apply became a replica CP with `applied` and `landed` ages reported separately; credits became units of free receiver staging; the transport session became per aggregate pair with relationships multiplexed by `rel`; direct attach became one SVM per port with a per-SVM WWNN; `no_path_retry queue` was withdrawn; LUN growth became the standby-first re-create; the corrupt-source loop gained the three-strikes re-read. The FC positions stand, corrected on LUN 0, `emulate_write_cache`, the nvfail presentation and the NPIV topology.

### 3.8 Revised Phase B recommendation

The stream items 0 to 2 (format decision, `otap-stream` v1, loopback shipper and receiver) are Phase C weeks 1 and 11 (Part 8). The FC items 3 to 8 (HBA inventory and boot profile, `blade_fc`, the Proxmox recipe, NPIV per SVM, transition under load, reboot persistence) move to Phase B step 1 (R46). Phase B also adds the stream on real links (1.0 GB/s at 10GbE with TLS, `ZC_COPIED` under 1% on a physical NIC, fallback on a tap-port peer), compression (200 MB/s raw over 1GbE at 2.5:1 with zstd), fallback and resume (100 cable pulls at 80% of line rate, every delta completes, resume within 3 s, zero duplicate applies, a sender crash before its superblock yields exactly one discarded partial), cursor persistence and both ages on the heartbeat, the timer rows handed to `otap-ctl`, byte-identical FC trees with the partner group Standby, and the nvfail lab on ESXi (unmap plus serial change with the dead node's paths present ends in a new device, never a resumed one). Phase A adds PR for FC LUNs with APTPL under a `dbroot` both nodes can read, `pr_survival` for WSFC-class initiators, and the shared-shelf standby presenting the same serial and capacity.

The inbox ESXi 8 qlnativefc binding for ISP2532 is unverified. `max_npiv_vports` is a firmware value and switch login limits per F_Port are [believed]. The relay on a Pi 4 under rustls is unmeasured; the homelab critic's arithmetic (30 messages per second of 1 KiB on a Cortex-A72) says it does not matter and the "Pi 5 recommended" line is deleted.

## Part 4. Opus-Consensus (quorum, failure domains, failover, SVM mobility)

### 4.1 Executive take

The control plane leaves the write path entirely; a client write consults one seqlock page and nothing else. Leases and epochs become a pure ownership and fencing mechanism; the degraded apparatus collapses into a protection report; and what the control plane must get right moves to the failover decision itself, because with private disks every unplanned failover promotes a copy that is behind the acknowledged state. The answer is to always promote and let lag decide the posture per volume: served, fenced under a new identity, or held until an operator clears, with a held promotion reversible if the old owner returns intact. The SVM is the unit that moves, so migration is a choreography of namespaces, mounts, daemons, addresses and grace periods. Single-node-first means the control plane costs nothing in Phase C. And the witness becomes a relay: a severed inter-node link with a live witness is a non-event.

### 4.2 Libraries and kernel interfaces

Crates (C32, C39): openraft 0.9.25 (pin `"0.9"`; 0.10 is alpha; `enable_elect`, `snapshot_policy`, `max_in_snapshot_log_to_keep` on `Config`; `save_vote` and `append` must persist before returning), redb 4.3.0 (`Durability::Immediate` persists at commit; 2.6.4 is the maintenance line), tokio 1.53.2, rustls 0.23.45, postcard 1.1.3, axum 0.8.9 (management API, not in v0.1), rtnetlink 0.23.0, genetlink 0.3.0, nix 0.31.3 or rustix 1.1.5, zbus 5.19.0, toml 1.1.8, proptest 1.12.0, madsim 0.2.34, turmoil 0.7.2, stateright 0.31.0, loom 0.7.2. Kernel and platform: `CONFIG_INET_DIAG_DESTROY=y` in Debian's 6.12.111-1, `tcp_abort()` sending an active reset for established and half-closed sockets, `ss -K`; `arping -D` returning 0 when DAD succeeded (no replies); `boot_id` as a UUID generated once per boot; `target_wwn_vpd_unit_serial_store` returning `-EINVAL` while `export_count` is non-zero; `VMW_SATP_ALUA action_OnRetryErrors` off by default so a retryable error leaves the path On and the I/O retried indefinitely (KB 310442); vSphere HA VMCP responses for PDL and APD disabled by default; ESXi flagging a snapshot LUN when the identity does not match VMFS metadata; ONTAP nvfail returning ESTALE to NFS clients and bringing LUNs offline; sg3-utils 1.48-3~deb13u1 (`sg_persist`) for Phase A; fence-agents 4.16.0-3 for a confirmed power fence.

### 4.3 Architecture

**Units and the failure matrix.** The SVM is the unit of ownership, lease, epoch, relationship (consistency group: root plus data volumes, on one aggregate) and mobility; the aggregate is the unit of CP and, in Phase A, of media ownership; the transport session is per aggregate pair with relationships multiplexed by `rel`. Raft membership `{A, B, W}` on openraft 0.9.25, W with `enable_elect = false`; leases granted after `ensure_linearizable()` with no log write and renewed in one batched RPC per node per 500 ms; `heartbeat_interval` 100 ms; election 1 to 2 s (at least ten times the witness RTT through the relay); `RaftNetwork` keeps two routes per target (direct LAN, then DAC; `CtlMsg::Relay` through W) for AppendEntries, Vote and lease RPC, never the stream. The three-way rule: a partner silent on every direct path is dead only if W is reachable and has not heard it for at least 2 s either; if W still hears it, it is a partition and the relay takes over; if W is unreachable too, it is no quorum. The eleven-row matrix (W down; DAC down; LAN switch dead; A-B severed with both reaching W; A isolated; A dead; A wedged; no quorum; W and A together; W paused; A-B flapping) is in the record (Consensus round 1, section 2.1) with two edits: "A isolated" ends in a promotion whose posture the gate decides, and "A-B severed, both reach W" is a non-event for ownership and a `Lagging` event for protection. The install guide's one hard recommendation follows from the matrix: a direct DAC carrying both the stream and a control path turns "switch dead" into "witness down".

**Failover: always promote, lag decides the posture.** `takeover_posture(svm, lag, policy, live) -> Posture`: within `nvfail_force_lag` (60 s) each volume keeps its class posture; beyond it, or when lag is `Unknown` (a `boot_id` mismatch on the replica superblock, or no heartbeat ever received), every volume of the SVM is `Hold`. A held promotion acknowledges nothing and is therefore reversible: if A returns with an intact superblock before `nvfail clear`, Raft commits `Revert{svm, to: A, epoch e+2}`, A resumes from its own head (losing its DRAM window, handled by Phase C's dirty-boot nvfail) and B resyncs from base K; auto-revert is the default because it never loses more than clearing would. `TakeoverPending` is deleted; the irreversible decision is `nvfail clear`. Lag is `(now_mono - recv_mono) + ship_duration + (send_mono - cut_mono)`, with `recv_mono` refreshed only by live heartbeats and paired with `boot_id`. The ladder when A dies at 0: silence 2 s; election 1.0 to 2.2 s if A led; L1 or L2 at 4.3 s p50, 5.3 s p99; gate under 1 ms from the replica superblock and `RelLive`, no RPC, no wall clock; commit at about 4.5 s with W's append fsync on the path; exports at 5 s p50, 9 s p99 (`Off` and `Fence` LUNs Active/Optimized; `Hold` LUNs unlinked, with the impersonation experiment in Phase B; NAS plus 2 to 5 s on clean logs, `Fence` volumes under a new fsid). The wedged-A ladder (11 to 13 s, hard 15 s), the returning node's roles (`Fenced` at boot; `Owner` when Raft's `(owner, epoch)` equals its superblock; `Divergent` otherwise, head pinned as `divergent-e<epoch>-cp<K+m>`, map rolled back to base K) and resync rules R-a to R-f stand: the Raft owner at the current epoch is always the source; destination content newer than the common base is pinned then discarded; the common base is the newest CP the source knows the destination applied and still holds; a planned migration hands over at a CP boundary so its resync is instant; direction reverses only through a planned migration; resync rides the ordinary stream.

**nvfail postures.** The table is in 6.6.

**Protection and relationship state.** Protection per SVM relationship is computed from the 1 s heartbeat and never written to Raft: `Single`, `Protected`, `Exposed` (lag over `insync_lag_max` or link dead 3 s; WARN sustained 30 s), `Exposed(CRIT)` (lag over `nvfail_force_lag`: "a failure of this node now holds every volume of SVM X until you clear"; hourly reminder), `Unprotected` (`NoCommonBase`). `Relationship { ver, id, svm, src, dst, src_epoch, policy, state }` in Raft carries no base: the two superblocks' snapshot tables hold it, and `otapctl rel show` reads it from the engine. `RelState { Baseline, Idle, Quiesced, Resync { from, to }, NoCommonBase { reason } }`; everything but `Baseline` and `NoCommonBase` promotes; `Resync` promotes from the newest complete CP of the current epoch, held. `RelLive` (never in Raft) carries `src_committed_cp`, `dst_applied_cp`, `cut_mono_newest`, `cut_mono_oldest_unshipped`, `unshipped_bytes`, `acked_bytes` per volume, `dirty_vols`, `land_rate_bytes_s`, `apply_stalled_ms`, `link`, `recv_boot_id`, `recv_mono`; the replica superblock records `applied_cp_seq`, `applied_owner_epoch`, `recv_boot_id`, `recv_mono`, `sender_cut_wall_ns`, `ship_duration_ms` and `acked_cp` in the same FUA write as the apply.

**Mobility and LIFs.** Block: `BlockStandby` from Phase B; each SVM has one block LIF per node inside its netns, identical ALUA group ids and `vpd_unit_serial` on both nodes, Standby on the replica; the planned cutover writes 15 (Transitioning) to the old group, `Immediate` commit on the receiver, 0 (Active/Optimized) to the new group, 2 (Standby) to the old. NAS LIFs move with the SVM and are announced by `nbladed`'s GARP and NA; the `arping -D` gate precedes an unplanned raise. `SvmMobility` (`ensure_standby`, `drain`, `activate`, `impersonate_block_lif`, `end_grace`) is in Part 7. Planned sequence: P0 preflight vetoes (relationship not Steady or lag over one CP, a CP older than 2 s in flight, landing-rate convergence failing, GC urgent, an S3 job running, open SMB handles above a threshold, business hours); P1 pre-copy to at most 256 MiB or one final round under the 2 s hold when the delta is at most 2 s times `land_rate`; P2 drain (smbd cgroup SIGTERM, `ss -K` on NAS LIF sockets, NAS LIFs down, versitygw SIGTERM, nfsd threads 0, clean unmounts); P3 to P7 the block half (Transitioning, final CP 0.6 to 0.9 s on HDD RAID6 for 256 MiB, ship, `Immediate` apply 0.3 to 0.5 s, Raft 0.1 to 0.25 s, ALUA flip 0.1 to 0.3 s: 1.5 to 2.5 s p50, 3 s cap); P8 to P10 NAS start on B (mounts, `THREADS_SET` with the scope, smbd, versitygw, LIFs plus GARP, `sm-notify -v`, early grace end): NFSv3 2 to 8 s, NFSv4.1 at most 8 s on reclaimed state; P11 SMB 10 to 20 s with open handles lost. A `Stuck` at the unmount aborts the move before any ownership change (A back in about 1 s). Unplanned: B promotes at the applied CP, flips its group to Active/Optimized, raises NAS LIFs after the gate, applies the per-class posture, and withholds every held NFS export.

**Slot table and Raft content.** `/run/opentap/slots`: 1,024 slots of 64 B, `MAP_SHARED`, written by `otap-ctl` only: `kind (Svm | Agg)`, `id`, `epoch`, `role (Single | Owner | Replica | Divergent | Fenced)`, `state (Owned | Standby | Transitioning | Fenced)`, `lease_expiry_mono_ns`, `gate_seq`, `nvfail_seq`, `protection`. The check at submission and completion is role in `{Single, Owner}`, state `Owned`, now before expiry (no lease in `Single`), about 5 ns [believed; measured in Phase C]. In Phase B each ublk device knows its SVM's slot index at creation (R39). Raft holds nodes, aggregates (Phase A), SVMs (`owner, home, epoch, mode: Serving | Migrating | Held{since} | Reverting | Down`, netns, protocols, slice, priority), volumes (`nvfail` posture, `size`, `size_max`, reservation), LIFs, LUN identities (`serial_gen`, `host_class`), relationships without a base, policies (snapshot, export, QoS groups, takeover), sealed secrets, the last 30 rehearsals, about 200 KiB; every `ClusterOp` carries an `actor` (R54). Not in Raft: leases, heartbeats, lag, bases and cursors, snapshot instances, nfsdcld, `secrets.tdb`, per-volume nvfail state, the divergent snapshot's contents, metrics, dirty indexes.

**No-quorum posture (D7).** `stop` in Phase B (the lease is the only arbiter between two private copies; under `freeze` one isolated node is a split brain waiting for the first fault), `freeze` in Phase A (PR arbitrates), `freeze` in Phase B only behind a confirmed power fence. Under `stop`: hold at 3 s, Transitioning, Unavailable at 13 s, resume on the next I/O after quorum, no epoch bump. What the clients see is in the timer table with the homelab correction: a single-path Proxmox host's `scsi_dh_alua` passes requests through Transitioning for `implicit_trans_secs` (10 s) and then flips the group to Standby, so its VMs get EIO at about 11 s; Linux multipath hosts at about 60 s; ESXi guests at 180 s with a VMware SCSI controller and open-vm-tools (30 s without the udev rule, 60 s for Windows guests); Windows iSCSI at 60 s. A 60 s witness reboot is survived by ESXi SCSI guests and NAS clients and by nothing else.

**Phase C and the witness.** Phase C is membership `{A}`: identical `ClusterState` types, zero network RPC, one slot read per I/O, fsync only on configuration changes, the watchdog and daemon-restart budgets kept, `otap-ctl` and `nbladed` at most 64 MiB each. The v0.1 control plane is redb behind a `StateStore` trait with the openraft implementation as Phase B's first item (R47, owner decision D3). The witness fsyncs on votes and appends only (steady-state heartbeats perform no storage call; a steady day is zero fsyncs); it stores the vote, the log, the `ClusterState` snapshot, its mTLS material, rehearsal records and the SVM placement table, never leases, lag, cursors or data; form factors are a Pi 4 or 5 on Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit (Debian 13 userland, the image's own kernel, 6.18 today, C38), an unprivileged LXC or a 1 vCPU VM, never on a data node's hypervisor host; the same `otap-ctl` cross-built for `aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu` (Tier 1) ships as `otap-witness_<ver>_arm64.deb`.

### 4.4 Bottlenecks

Catch-up is a window of exposure bounded by the replica's landing rate and PBA-sorted read-back (150 to 250 MB/s on aged HDD, so 360 GB is 25 to 40 minutes), during which lag exceeds 60 s and every volume would be held; the alert carries the measured rate and ETA. Lag without clocks errs by one heartbeat plus one RTT, toward "more lag". Relay latency against renewals (two witness RTTs at p99 200 ms against a 500 ms cadence; a witness RTT above 300 ms is refused at install). The witness fsync on the failover commit path (4.3 s becomes at worst 4.8 s on a cheap card). Survivor memory (both nodes' SVMs on one node; the per-node slice cap). XFS log replay on the replica at an unplanned NAS failover (receiver-side pre-replay is Phase B work; "15 to 60 s" until measured). GARP is ours to send; a switch with port security pins MACs. The replica's nfsdcld database is one CP old, so a client that first connected inside the last interval is refused reclaim and loses locks, not data. The nvfail list is "possibly affected" to within the last heartbeat, made exact by `acked_bytes` since the last shipped CP. Failover and return racing is idempotent. Clients with sessions to both nodes: the `ReqId` dedupe no longer spans nodes, which is fine because the replica never saw those requests. A transiently slow witness is absorbed up to TTL. A paused data-node VM re-reads `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` after every blocking call; the witness's clock is never used.

### 4.5 Creative solutions

The witness as a relay, which turns the severed inter-node link into a non-event. Always-promote with a reversible held posture, which moves the irreversible decision to `nvfail clear` and makes "wait for A" free. Lag from `cut_mono` and `boot_id`, so neither wall clocks nor a replica reboot can lie to the gate. The three-way dead, partition, no-quorum rule from four evidence sources. The per-SVM slot page checked at submission and completion. The empty-log witness (fsyncs only on votes and appends). The `Divergent` role with the pinned divergent head, which is SnapMirror's `-preserve` as the default.

### 4.6 Holes Consensus found in the others

In Storage: the ublk data path as written did not exist on 6.12 (C03); a snapshot under continuous ingest held the NAS freeze for the whole CP drain; shipping stripe images verbatim shipped GC relocations and other volumes' blocks (`kind` bits and per-group packing); nvfail-on-promote in `precise` mode could bring an unsafe volume online (the conservative promotion set); intent writes collided with the four-slot superblock ring (separate intent slots); the RAID6 write hole under a sub-stripe metadata stream (full-stripe everything, C21); hardware RAID commands are 128 or 256 KiB and write-through is scheduled, not a fault (probe from 64 KiB, `max_hw_sectors_kb`, the write-policy event); md over single-drive VDs loses the flush chain and skipped the attestation (members attested); the arena arithmetic (CP interval is min(timer, 4 GiB divided by ingest)); `nvfail = on` for the root took every SVM down on every dirty boot (root class with the content check). In Protocols: the namespace pins could not be created as described (C16, a blocker fixed by `unshare --fork`); one `lockd` thread serves every SVM (C13); the planned move shipped after it stopped (pre-copy veto, portals closed before addresses); every Phase B NAS failover pays XFS log replay; BUILTIN SIDs; the NFSv4 scope welded to the NetBIOS name (cluster-unique string); the NFS nvfail row mislabelled (removing the export gives ESTALE at once) and the clear gap (`rollback_gen` in the fsid from day one); nfsdcld must hold the pipe within one second. In Fabrics: the stream was specified against a delta Storage did not produce; `tls = false` contradicted R21 and left the receiver unauthenticated; `Committed(cp)` meant two things; credits were granted for landing not on disk; standby paths versus LIF moves; a corrupt source re-sent forever.

### 4.7 What the review changed

Consensus accepted every hole raised against it: failover is never gated on lag and the gate decides the posture; `TakeoverPending` and `max_auto_takeover_lag` are deleted in favour of `nvfail_force_lag`; ALUA Unavailable is never called "PDL-style"; a backstore stall is a Storage-reported condition and never a failover trigger (the 5 s self-takeover is withdrawn); age is measured from `cut_mono`; slots, leases, epochs and placement are per SVM; pre-copy converges against the landing rate; the file class defaults to `Fence`; the warm-standby skeleton is Protocols' pin set entered with `nsenter`; the scope is written through genetlink from inside the netns and guarded by the UTS nodename; the clear reminder runs every 30 s for ten minutes then every five; `Disk.PathEvalTime` is a revival accelerator, never a guard; `DeltaSink::end(commit: Immediate)` and the generation ring are prerequisites for the cutover budget; one `LifState` with a transport body; one memory table with the survivor rule; the heartbeat carries `acked_bytes` since the last shipped CP; `(boot_id, recv_mono)` on the replica superblock; the no-quorum row carries the guest timers; `base` left Raft; the root volume gets `Fence`; the arena owner process is struck. It rejected `PathEvalTime` as the resume bound after `stop` (ALUA Unavailable paths stay On and resume on the next I/O, KB 310442), which the fact check supports.

### 4.8 Revised Phase C recommendation

Nine work items (record, Consensus round 3, section 4), of which v0.1 builds the single-node state store, the watchdog with data-path check-ins and dirty-boot nvfail, daemon restart through `USER_RECOVERY`, the node-local half of the SVM lifecycle and LIF manager, the declarative state and reconciler, and alerts and metrics; the Apalache model, the three-voter madsim harness, the arm64 witness lane and the membership dry run are Phase B's first week, because they do not depend on Phase C code (R47, R48). Phase B adds the witness and relay in production, leader leases L1 to L4 per SVM, the relationship and protection state machines, the posture gate with `(boot_id, recv_mono)`, `Hold`, impersonation, `Revert` and both clear paths, `BlockStandby`, warm-standby pins, resync rules and the divergent snapshot, with the exit tests listed in the record. Phase A adds `ScsiPr` and `ShelfMailbox`, the `freeze` default, aggregate slots load-bearing, md assembly on failover, the stripe-cache drain test and lossless takeover when the shelf holds the last CP.

### 4.9 Risks Consensus carries forward

The 5 ns slot check and the relay's cost on a Pi are [believed] until measured. Whether a held promotion writes nothing beyond the e+1 superblock is a requirement on the engine (R29), not yet an exit test. The impersonation of a dead node's block LIF to obtain PDL on ESXi is a Phase B experiment with an undocumented mixed-path case (C01). The `stop` posture's cost on single-path Proxmox hosts (every VM errors at about 11 s) is the number the owner decides D7 with.

### 6.1 The product, as it will be built

**Phase C, v0.1 (12 weeks, two engineers).** One Debian 13 node. Aggregates on mdadm RAID1, 5 or 6 over an IT-mode HBA, or on a single NVMe or SATA device; a hardware-RAID virtual drive only through manual geometry and an operator-attested write-back cache. One or more SVMs, each three Linux namespaces with a pivoted view, a root volume and a junction tree. iSCSI LUNs (`Native4k`, and `Esxi512e` by shadow read-modify-write) with ALUA and TPGS advertised from the first LUN under vendor `LIO-ORG`, product `OpenTAP`. NFSv3 datastore SVMs. Crash-consistent snapshots with a schedule, retention and clone LUNs. nvfail in conservative intent mode with the four-fact printout. `otapctl volume dump --since <snap>` to a file and `restore`, which is both the backup feature and the replication bridge. `otapctl` over a local socket, Prometheus metrics, `.deb` packages from an apt repository, an installer that reproduces a node from `otapctl config export`. A `lab` profile that runs in a 4 vCPU, 8 GB VM. Stretch, in order, as v0.1.x point releases with no format change: the per-aggregate PLP-NVMe durable log, NAS snapshot exposure with Previous Versions and `volume snapshot restore`, the SMB standalone share class, the NFSv4.1 file SVM, precise nvfail intent slots, the `Esxi512e` patch path, the metadata class, the QoS bucket, the hardware-RAID VD path with attestation.

**Phase B, v0.2.** Two nodes with internal drives, a witness and the asynchronous stream. Every SVM's consistency group ships every CP to the partner over mTLS at up to 1.0 GB/s on 10GbE; a replica is always at a CP boundary. Fibre Channel client targets land here as step 1. Unplanned failover promotes the replica in 5 s p50 and 9 s p99 for block, and lag decides each volume's posture: served, fenced under a new identity, or held until an operator clears, reversibly. Planned failover is 1.5 to 2.5 s for block, 2 to 8 s for NFSv3, under 8 s for NFSv4.1 on reclaimed state, 10 to 20 s for SMB with open handles lost. S3 with Veeam Object Lock arrives when a tagged versitygw release passes the Veeam test. AD-joined SMB, NPIV FC LIFs, QoS policy groups on SVMs, `volume rehost`, encryption at rest, RBAC.

**Phase A, v0.3.** The same pair on a shared SAS shelf with SCSI-3 persistent reservations and the `freeze` posture; a takeover of the shelf is lossless only with the priced `ShelfSsdRing`.

**The honest sentence about loss.** On one node, a power cut loses the unflushed arena: one CP, min(10 s, 4 GiB divided by ingest) on HDD, 5 s on flash; a guest `fsync` changes nothing because FLUSH is an ordering barrier, not a disk barrier, unless the volume's aggregate has the durable log. On two nodes, an unplanned failover rolls back by the applied age plus the lease: 8 to 19 s on an HDD pair at 300 MB/s on 10GbE, up to 25 s under load before anyone is warned, and every volume is held rather than served once the lag passes 60 s. The product prints the window, the bytes, the volumes and the command after every such event. "15 s" is the brochure; this paragraph is the documentation.

### 6.2 Rulings

Round 2's R1 to R24 stand except where re-ruled below. Two amendments to standing rulings: R13 (`qlini_mode`) becomes `disabled` on every port, because FC is not a transport and no port is ever an initiator; R22's "our scrub replaces `checkarray`" is withdrawn (R60).

**R25. One format decision before any code: the envelope is Fabrics', the payload is Storage's.** `otap-stream` v1 freezes the 64-byte frame, the record kinds, the cursor and the credit semantics and treats the payload as opaque bytes with a CRC. `otap-format` v2 freezes the stripe image (summary page with the 2-bit `kind` and the per-stripe volume bitmap, then data) as the only chunk payload, together with the superblock, the intent slot, the CP log record, the map page and page index, the segment table (with a per-segment `geom` field) and the snapshot table. A chunk is re-derivable from disk by `(lseg, stripe_idx)`; chunk-aligned resume is the contract; the delta's segments are GC-pinned from `describe()` to `DeltaEnd`. Both freeze in week 1 with golden vectors, `const` layout assertions and reserved zeroed fields covered by the CRC, so Phase B and Phase 3 land without a major bump.

**R26. On parity pools every write is a whole stripe, and no journal is mandated.** Data stripes, log-structured metadata segments, the four superblock slots and the two intent slots are each written as one full stripe with FUA, so a torn write damages only uncommitted rows and a forced dirty-degraded assembly is safe for committed data. `--write-journal` is not required (its bitmap exclusion would turn a two-minute resync into twenty hours) and PPL is refused because it forwards no flush. The week-5 forced dirty-degraded test decides: if a committed block is ever lost, the journal becomes mandatory for RAID6 and the bitmap is dropped (D9). Phase A follows the same result.

**R27. One nvfail vocabulary and one class table.** The posture enum is `NvPosture { Hold, Fence, Off }`: `Hold` serves nothing on the volume until `nvfail clear`; `Fence` serves at once under a new identity (a new NFS `fsid` generation, an smbd restart, a new LUN serial where the host class requires it) and raises the CRIT with the window; `Off` serves the old identity and is for lab volumes. The class table in 6.6 is the owner's decision D5, presented once. Per volume: an explicit posture override and a `force_hold` flag (held on every promotion regardless of lag, the `-dr-force-nvfail` analogue). The clear is per volume, with `--svm` as the convenience; it never runs automatically on a datastore class, with or without a UPS. The intent mode is `conservative` in v0.1 (every writable online volume counts as active); `precise` is a stretch item. The documentation states that ONTAP's default is off and why OpenTAP's is not.

**R28. Identity after an unplanned failover follows the host class.** `LunIdentity { serial_gen, host_class: Esxi | Linux | Windows | Mixed, policy: NewIdentity | SameIdentity }`. `Esxi`, `Windows` and `Mixed` default to `NewIdentity`: on clear the LUN returns with `vpd_unit_serial = <uuid>-g<gen>` (hence a new NAA), a new LUN number and the old mapping removed, which ESXi treats as a snapshot LUN to resignature and nothing resumes silently. `Linux` defaults to `SameIdentity`: unmap, clear, remap under the same serial, because a Linux host's `scsi_dh_alua` fails requests on Standby and Unavailable paths at once and an unmapped LUN gives EIO at once, so there is no silent resume to prevent, while a new serial breaks Proxmox volume IDs and LVM bases. FC LUNs are always `NewIdentity` because an FC LIF cannot be impersonated. The Consensus experiment (the survivor answers 25h/00h on the dead node's block LIF so ESXi reaches PDL and the LUN returns under the same identity) is Phase B work item B7, gated on the ESXi lab; until it passes, the ESXi default stays `NewIdentity` and the documentation never says "PDL" for the mixed dead-path case (C01). Single-node dirty boots and planned cases use unmap and remap under the same identity for every class, with `nvfail clear` refusing to remap an ESXi LUN until PDL is confirmed on every registered host (Phase B, with the host registry).

**R29. Always promote; lag decides the posture; a held promotion is reversible.** `takeover_posture` applies each volume's class posture within `nvfail_force_lag` (60 s) and `Hold` to every volume beyond it or when lag is `Unknown`. The engine commits no CP containing client writes for a held volume, so a promotion in which every volume is held writes nothing beyond the e+1 superblock; if the old owner returns with an intact superblock before any clear, `Revert{svm, to: A, e+2}` names it owner without a rollback and the survivor resyncs from the common base. Once any volume of the SVM has acknowledged a write on the survivor, the returning node is `Divergent` and the resync rules R-a to R-f apply. Storage's engine gains the exit test "a held promotion acknowledges nothing and writes no data CP".

**R30. One lag model and one set of thresholds.** `applied_age_s` is measured from the target CP's `cut_mono` and is the gate's number; `landed_age_s` is for the dashboard; the operator sees one number called lag in `h:m:s`. `insync_lag_max` is the CP timer plus the maximum ship plus the apply: 25 s on HDD, 15 s on flash. `Exposed` WARN fires when lag exceeds `insync_lag_max` for 30 s or the link is dead for 3 s; `Exposed(CRIT)` when lag exceeds `nvfail_force_lag` (60 s), with an hourly reminder; `Unprotected` at `NoCommonBase`. Storage's "warn 60 s, crit 600 s" and `max_auto_takeover_lag` are purged from every text. The receiver forces a CP on `CpEnd` when none is in flight and runs a 2 s timer while a relationship is inbound; cutovers and promotions use `commit: Immediate`.

**R31. The staging pool is the shipper's source and the hold is released on copy.** A staged ingest stripe is released when its disk write has completed and the transport has encrypted it into its bounce buffer; the bounce half is released at the send completion (`NOTIF` for zero-copy sends, the send CQE otherwise). Any later resend reads the stripe back through `DeltaSource::read_chunk` under `MediaProfile.ship_readback` (default `cache`; the shipper never issues I/O to the HDD pool itself). `Landed` credits govern flow only and are units of free receiver staging. The arena is never a send source and never registered for sends.

**R32. Sizes and reservations.** A NAS volume's ublk device is created at `size_max` (thin, map-backed) and its XFS at `size`; growth raises `size` and runs `xfs_growfs` with no device re-create, and the engine refuses writes beyond `size`. A client-visible LUN presents `size` and grows by an ALUA-rolled re-create (standby first, group flip, then the other side; a planned 3 to 5 s pause on a single node). Reservation is an aggregate accounting policy per volume, `Thick | Thin`, with file volumes thick by default (R18) and LUNs thin; thin-LUN thresholds through VPD B2h are Phase B. `df` on a client therefore shows `size`. Consensus's `ensure_standby` pre-creates replica devices at `size_max` for NAS and at `size` for LUNs.

**R33. One memory table, owned by Storage.** The table in 6.4 is the only memory budget. The SVM slice cap is derived: Phase C `svm.slice MemoryMax` is DRAM minus the engine budget minus 4 GiB for the host, 30 GiB on a 64 GB node; Phase B's per-node cap is half of the survivor's figure, 16 GiB on 64 GB, so both nodes' SVMs fit one survivor, and SVMs beyond the cap start in `priority` order with an alert rather than being refused. Every size is a profile value computed by the installer.

**R34. One scope string, one writer.** The NFSv4 server scope (and therefore `so_major_id`) is `<svm-name>.<cluster-uuid8>`, fixed at SVM creation as a token in Raft and never changed by a rename, because it must be identical on both nodes forever and unique across clusters. `otap-nsinit` sets it as the UTS nodename; `nfsdctl autostart` passes it as `NFSD_A_SERVER_SCOPE` at every cold start; every later thread change is issued from inside the UTS namespace without a scope attribute, so `nfsd_svc`'s rewrite reproduces the same string (C14); `THREADS_GET` reads it back as the post-condition. Samba's `netbios name` is the 15-character SVM name set explicitly in `smb.conf`.

**R35. R7 stands under a controller's write-through window.** The 2 s per-request cap and ALUA Transitioning are the whole of the back-pressure contract; the CP timer is never lengthened and the threshold never raised for a backstore condition. What changes is attribution: `drain_rate_bytes_s` is published, the WARN after three CPs below half the probed rate names the three likely causes, and the attestation's `learn_mode` lets the alert say "known write-through window". The structural fix is the hardware guidance (R50): an HBA-mode controller has no window. The owner may revisit after the week-4 PERC measurement (D8).

**R36. NAS unplanned failover is "15 to 60 s" until measured.** The receiver-side pre-replay (per applied CP: writable clone, mount, let XFS replay, snapshot as the promotable root, discard the clone, off the client path) is a Phase B work item owned by Storage, measured for 20 volumes with 128 MiB logs with and without pre-replay; the alternative (every N-th CP fsfreeze-cut) is kept until the measurement. Planned moves avoid replay through the clean unmount.

**R37. One persistent MAC per NAS LIF, and the `arping -D` gate.** Per-(LIF, node) MACs are rejected because a MAC change makes every failover depend on clients accepting an unsolicited ARP, which Windows is not known to do. The survivor raises a NAS LIF on an unplanned failover only after `arping -D -c 3 -w 1` reports silence, with the watchdog deadline as the backstop, so a wedged node costs at most 8 s. The C2 exit test measures the wedged-node pause.

**R38. Receiver staging is 2 GiB per inbound pipe** in the memory table, with the landing index at 16 B per block; credits adapt; Fabrics' minimum of 64 MiB at 10GbE and 256 MiB at 25GbE is satisfied.

**R39. The SVM is the unit; the slot is per SVM.** Slots, leases, epochs, placement and relationship state are per SVM; the aggregate is the unit of CP and, in Phase A only, of media ownership. Every ublk device records its SVM's slot index at creation and the engine's epoch check at submission and completion reads that slot; in Phase C there is one slot. The default consistency group is the SVM's volumes, root included, on one aggregate; a volume on another node's aggregate is refused.

**R40. The promotion set is "class posture unless provably idle".** Every writable volume of the promoted SVM takes its class posture unless two consecutive `CpBegin`s showed its bit clear and no later heartbeat reported a write; `write_active` and `acked_bytes` size the alert, and the printout lists volumes with `acked_bytes > 0` as affected and the rest of `write_active` as "idle, bit not yet cleared".

**R41. Block protocols are SVM protocols with standby paths from Phase B.** Each SVM's iSCSI target is instantiated on both nodes of a pair inside the SVM's netns with one block LIF per node and one ALUA group per node; block LIFs never move; every block cutover, planned or unplanned, is the ALUA flip; NAS LIFs move with the SVM. Data LUNs are 1 to 255 and a data LUN at mapped LUN 0 is refused per (TPG, ACL); `demo_mode_discovery=0` and `generate_node_acls=0` on every TPG; portals are cluster-unique and never wildcard; `vendor LIO-ORG / product OpenTAP` is permanent; `emulate_write_cache` is never written and the ublk device's `write_cache` and `fua` are asserted before any LUN link. R9's "pause at most 3 s planned" becomes 1.5 to 2.5 s p50 with a 3 s cap (D13).

**R42. No-quorum posture: `stop` in Phase B, `freeze` in Phase A, prepared for D7.** Under `stop` a node that loses quorum holds at 3 s, goes Transitioning, Unavailable at 13 s, writes a final local CP at TTL, and resumes on the next I/O when quorum returns with no epoch bump. `freeze` in Phase B is permitted only behind a confirmed power fence (fence-agents through iDRAC, IPMI, Redfish or a PDU). The row in the timer table carries the client and guest numbers, including the single-path Proxmox host whose VMs error at about 11 s.

**R43. Snapshots as user-visible objects land in v0.1.x, not Phase B.** v0.1 ships crash-consistent snapshots, schedules, retention, autodelete (`try` and `disrupt`, never `destroy`) and clone LUNs. The first stretch items add `Fsfreeze` snapshots with `cut_within`, read-only ublk devices over pinned roots, explicit per-snapshot NFS exports with `fsid = hash(vol_uuid, snap_uuid)`, `.snapshot` at the volume root under the junction (SMB root-and-junction-only behaviour), `vfs_shadow_copy2` with an absolute `shadow:snapdir`, the automount cap of eight per SVM with a 300 s idle timeout as an exit-test item (64 volumes with 35 snapshots are 2,240 potential mounts), `otapctl volume snapshot restore` with the replica rolling back to the same snapshot and `--force` past the base raising a CRIT, and copy-restore for files with its cost printed. Block-sharing file restore is Phase 3.

**R44. Vocabulary.** "Failover" and "failback" for every Phase B and Phase C event; "takeover" and "giveback" only for lossless Phase A events with `ShelfSsdRing`. The relationship state `Broken` is `NoCommonBase`. Autodelete levels are `try` and `disrupt`. Help text prints the ONTAP equivalent of every verb.

**R45. The durable log returns as the first post-v0.1 item.** `LocalNvmeRing` (per-record FUA to a PLP NVMe, 10 to 20 us added to each acknowledgement on Optane-class media) is a per-aggregate opt-in `durable_log = "<device>"`, off by default, whose volumes print a loss window of 0 s. v0.1 reserves its format (the slot-state page, `CpTrigger::UnmirroredFlush`, a `Backstore` for the ring) in week 1 so it lands as v0.1.1 without a format change. Owner decision D2 is whether to trade NFSv3 for it inside the 12 weeks.

**R46. Fibre Channel and S3 leave v0.1.** The owner's protocol sequence (iSCSI, then NFS and SMB, then FC) is kept as a sequence; FC client targets become Phase B step 1 because nothing in them is reachable from CI and the kernel-side facts are now verified, so building them early retires no design risk (D1). FC as a replication transport is not built in any phase. S3 arrives in v0.1.x when a tagged versitygw release containing PR #2479 passes the Veeam immutability test; until then the product promises no immutability.

**R47. The v0.1 control plane is redb behind a `StateStore` trait.** `ClusterState` and `ClusterOp` are the same types in every phase and the on-disk snapshot format (postcard, versioned) is frozen in v0.1; the openraft 0.9 implementation of `StateStore` is Phase B's first week, and the upgrade imports the snapshot into `Raft::initialize({a})` before `add_learner` and `change_membership`. This departs from round 2's byte-identical control plane for schedule and is owner decision D3. The watchdog, the data-path check-ins, the slot page and dirty-boot nvfail are in v0.1 regardless.

**R48. The Phase C plan is the implementer's 12-week table, amended.** Two engineers; each week ends with its exit test green in CI; a red test stops new work. Amendments: week 1 adds the Raft object split, the snapshot-format freeze and the durable-log reservation; week 9 adds the cluster-state region in the aggregate; week 12 adds the apt repository, the installer and the documentation set; the stretch list is ordered as in 6.1. The four personas' Phase C lists are re-labelled Phase C, v0.1.x, Phase B and Phase 3 in Part 8.

**R49. Profiles and host shapes.** `profile = production | lab`. `lab` runs in a 4 vCPU, 8 GB VM: a 1 GiB arena, no isolated cores, no busy-poll, manual geometry allowed, a 128 MiB arena in CI. `production` defaults to the wake-up mode (8 to 13 us) with busy-polled reactors as an opt-in (D12), because four spinning cores cost 30 to 50 W on a box idling at 110 W. Network shapes in order of recommendation: a single port, an active-backup bond, an LACP bond; MTU 1500 by default, 9000 recommended for datastores with a path-MTU check in `otap-host-check`. CHAP is optional. Windows 11 is a single-path client (no MPIO on the client edition); MPIO rows belong to Windows Server.

**R50. Hardware guidance.** The reference configuration is an IT-mode SAS3008 or SAS3108 HBA (HBA330, or a PERC 9 or later switched to HBA mode, where drives are pass-through with real WCE and relayed flushes) with mdadm. A PERC or MegaRAID virtual drive is the attested fallback: manual geometry or the random probe, the `cache_protection` struct with `disk_cache = Disabled` for every member type, `learn_mode`, and "attested, not verified" on every line of `pool show`. The VD rule reads: enable large-I/O support (1 MiB commands) where the firmware offers it and keep strip times data disks at or under the measured `max_sectors_kb`; in write-back the controller coalesces and the probe shows no knee, so the rule matters only in the write-through fallback. Boot disks are md RAID1 on two SSDs or a BOSS card. Member-drive health on a hardware VD is unmonitored without the vendor CLI, which is never a runtime dependency; the hardware guide says so.

**R51. Junction mount points.** Mount-point directories are root-owned, mode 000, with the immutable attribute; an export is added only after the mount is verified and removed before the unmount; children are unmounted with their parent; `otapctl volume show` prints the parenthesised state for an inactive junction; a nvfailed volume returns ESTALE and a planned move hangs clients so they retry. The C3 exit test includes an ESXi host writing to a junction whose volume is unmounted.

**R52. LIFs carry ONTAP's fields.** `LifSpec { id, svm, kind: Nas | Block { node }, transport: Ip { addr, vlan, mac } | Fc { wwpn, wwnn }, home_node, home_port, failover_group, failover_policy: LocalOnly | FollowSvm | Disabled, auto_revert }`; `current` is runtime state, never in Raft. A failover group is a set of `(node, port)` in one L2 domain with a reachability check; under a bond, a port is the bond. `otapctl lif revert` exists. The UI never shows a LIF on a node that does not own the data: "LIF failover" is port failover, and cross-node failover is an SVM event.

**R53. QoS is a named policy group.** `QosPolicyGroup { svm, name, max_iops, max_mbps, min_iops (reserved), is_shared: false }` is a Raft object assignable to volumes and LUNs in Phase C and to SVMs in Phase B; nested policies apply the most restrictive (two buckets in series); reads are metered; snapshot mounts inherit the parent's policy; the engine's catch-up share and scrub budget are subordinate to client QoS. The bucket runs in the ublk handler (1 ms refill, delay never fail, 1 s hold cap, `REQ_META` bypass) with per-bdi caps for NAS volumes. The documentation says OpenTAP meters storage I/O behind the page cache, not client operations. Implementation is a stretch item; the schema is week 1.

**R54. The Raft object model is split before it freezes.** SVM-owned objects (volumes, snapshots and their policies, LUNs, igroups, exports, shares, S3 users, DNS, QoS groups) carry an `svm` owner; cluster-owned objects (aggregates, nodes, ports, broadcast domains, LIF creation, relationships, witness) do not; LIFs and junctions are top-level objects so a LIF move does not rewrite the SVM. Every `ClusterOp` carries an `actor`. v0.1's `otapctl` speaks to `otap-ctl` over a local unix socket; the remote mTLS API with client certificates and RBAC (cluster admin, SVM admin with the vsadmin scope) is Phase B as a policy layer; the web UI is Phase 2.

**R55. Observability.** `otap-ctl` serves Prometheus text at the node level in `init_net`, aggregating `dbladed` and `nbladed` metrics over unix sockets and proxying scrapes of per-SVM daemons; the catalogue (Appendix of the operations guide) carries volume, SVM and relationship labels and covers arena occupancy, the throttle delay histogram, CP duration and bytes, `drain_rate_bytes_s`, QoS holds, per-LUN latency, nvfail state, posture, lag and both ages, `ZC_COPIED`, `cache_corruption`, ship and land rates, restart counts, stale `sm` entries, "root rewound". Logs are structured (`tracing`) to journald with a retention policy on the boot SSD. `otapctl support-bundle` collects the configfs tree, sysfs geometry, the attestation report, superblock and intent slots, CP-log statistics, relationship cursors, `lsns`, the client's `multipath -t`, and redacts secrets. One severity scale: INFO, WARN, CRIT.

**R56. Installer and first boot (week 12).** `.deb` packages from an apt repository on a clean Debian 13; `otapctl cluster create` generates the cluster key and CA, writes the platform profile and asks for the one reboot; `otapctl aggregate create` runs the probe on empty backstores and the attestation dialogue (printing the three vendor commands and parsing pasted output); `/etc/target/{pr,alua}` are created before `target_core_mod` loads; `rtslib-fb-targetctl.service` is masked if present; a default SVM and the admin credential are created; the first-boot health check asserts `write_cache == "write back"` and `fua == 1` on every ublk device. Exit: an unattended install from the `.deb` to a serving iSCSI LUN on an mdadm RAID6 and on a single NVMe. An ISO is v0.2.

**R57. Upgrade and rollback.** A `dbladed` package upgrade is the supervised restart through `USER_RECOVERY` with the same 2 s target and 5 s hard budget as a crash. A kernel update is the maintenance-reboot ladder: final CP, `run_state = CleanShutdown`, reboot, rebuild of the ublk devices and the target tree, clients seeing the planned-stop numbers. `unattended-upgrades` is pinned off for kernel and OpenTAP packages. No release writes a format that N-1 cannot read until the operator runs `otapctl format upgrade`.

**R58. Secrets, certificates, time, power.** `otapctl cluster create` generates `/etc/opentap/cluster.key` and the cluster CA (rcgen; ten-year CA, one-year node and witness certificates, rotation by `otapctl cert renew`), exports the CA for Veeam and browsers, and supports TPM2 sealing of the key through `systemd-cryptenroll`. `otapctl cluster export --with-key` is the backup. SVM S3 and SMB certificates are sealed in Raft. chrony is required and large steps alert; all SVMs share the node clock. UPS handling is two-stage through NUT: on battery, the CP timer drops to 2 s with a WARN and the node keeps serving; on low battery or after a configured runtime, final CP, stop exporting, `CleanShutdown`; `upsmon` calls `otapctl node shutdown --reason ups`; a clean shutdown never trips nvfail; the witness never shares a data node's UPS.

**R59. A pool is self-describing.** `otap-ctl` writes its `ClusterState` snapshot (under 1 MB) into a reserved aggregate region at every commit, and `otapctl aggregate import` on a fresh install rebuilds the node from the pool plus the restored cluster key. Format reservation in week 1, implementation in week 9 beside config export and import. Storage's Phase A "aggregate import and export" moves to Phase C for this reason.

**R60. SMART, scrubbing, replacement, growth.** A scheduled md `check` runs under the same read budget as catch-up and its `mismatch_cnt` alerts; `smartd` monitors members and NVMe; `xfs_scrub -n` runs on SVM filesystems; the engine's summary-driven scrub is kept for data CRCs. An md rebuild is a known drain cause and raises no WARN; hot spares, `sync_speed_max` and `mdadm --monitor` are wired into the alerting. The per-segment `geom` field is in the format before the freeze so a reshape never changes old segments. Pool growth is Phase B; drive replacement is a v0.1 runbook.

**R61. Encryption.** At rest: `dm-crypt` above md (the probe runs on the dm device, the attestation on the physical devices), shipped in Phase B after `crypt_map`'s flush and FUA pass-through is verified; `cryptsetup` with TPM2 enrolment is in-box. In flight: the stream and the control plane are mTLS; S3 has a per-SVM certificate; `smb encrypt = desired`; the documentation states that NFS `sec=sys` and iSCSI with CHAP are plaintext until Kerberos NFS and NVMe/TCP TLS arrive, and that the arena and staging are plaintext by design.

**R62. `throttle_kbps` per pipe and per endpoint.** Unlimited on the direct link by default; set by the operator on the LAN endpoint; applied at the next chunk.

**R63. The replication operator surface.** `otapctl rel show` prints one line per relationship: lag in `h:m:s`, state, status (Idle, Transferring, Quiesced), healthy, the unhealthy reason, the last transfer's size and duration. Verbs: `update`, `quiesce`, `abort`, `throttle`, `baseline`, `resync`. Phase B.

**R64. Clones.** Clone split is Phase C+1 (a re-birth of every shared block, throttled at 30% of read bandwidth like catch-up); clone-of-replica on the receiver while the relationship stays Steady is Phase B (the DR test); `snapshot show` carries an `owners` column so the admin sees why a snapshot will not delete.

Also ruled in passing: no arena owner process exists (the Phase C re-attach test proves it); `otap-ringd` is retired; the receiver lives inside `dbladed`; FC-NVMe is not a target or a transport; IPv6 LIFs are Phase B; the ipvlan VM profile is Phase 2 and labelled "pause up to the client's ARP timeout"; SR-IOV LIFs are a stretch after macvlan passes, with the mlx4 module-parameter caveat.

### 6.6 The nvfail table (owner decision D5) and the client's view

Postures: `Hold` serves nothing until `otapctl volume nvfail-clear`; `Fence` serves at once under a new identity and raises the CRIT with the window; `Off` serves the old identity (lab only). Identity on clear follows R28. ONTAP's default for its nvfail option is off; OpenTAP's defaults are on for datastore classes because a dirty boot is certain loss of up to one CP, not suspected loss.

| Volume class | Default posture | What the client sees at nvfail | On clear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Datastore LUN (iSCSI, FC) | `Hold` | LUN unmapped on every TPG: ESXi PDL (25h/00h on all live paths) on a single node or planned case; after an unplanned failover the dead node's paths keep ESXi in APD until clear or rescan; Linux single-path EIO at once; Linux multipath EIO about 60 s; Windows errors after 60 s | `Linux`: remap, same serial; `Esxi`, `Windows`, `Mixed`, FC: new serial `<uuid>-g<gen>`, new LUN number (ESXi resignatures, Linux sees a new WWID); the printout says "reset every VM on these LUNs, including the healthy-looking ones" |
| NFSv3 datastore | `Hold` | export withheld: ESTALE at once; ESXi datastore inaccessible, no self-remount (a rebooted host remounts by path and starts its VMs fresh, which is safe) | re-export with `fsid = <uuid>-g<gen>`; ESXi hosts unmount and remount with VMs powered off ("twenty minutes in vCenter") |
| File (NFSv4.1, SMB) | `Fence`, with an install-time acknowledgement that prints "up to 60 s of acknowledged NAS writes are discarded on an unplanned failover"; `Hold` if declined | NFS: new `fsid` generation, ESTALE drops cached state with EIO to applications; SMB: smbd restarted, handles gone, STATUS_BAD_NETWORK_NAME until the share returns; served within seconds | nothing; the CRIT carries the window |
| Object (S3) | `Fence` | versitygw restarted; Veeam retries; lock metadata is file metadata at the CP | nothing |
| SVM root volume | `Fence` with the content check | the SVM starts; clients that first connected in the lost interval get `RECLAIM_BAD` and re-open; a rotated machine password raises CRIT with the re-join command; WARN "root rewound" when `(cp_seq, rollback_gen)` differs from the last successful start | nothing |
| Any, operator override | `Off` | old identity, old fsid | lab only |

Per volume: `force_hold` holds the volume on every promotion regardless of lag. Promotion applies the posture to every writable volume unless provably idle (R40); beyond `nvfail_force_lag` or on `Unknown` lag every volume is `Hold` (R29). `stop` and ordinary failovers use ALUA Unavailable and Standby, never a PDL presentation. NOT READY (02h/04h) is never used because ESXi treats it as APD and resumes silently.

## Part 7. Standardized Rust trait contracts

Conventions. Every on-disk and on-wire structure is a `repr(C)` zerocopy view in `otap-format` (disk) or `otap-stream` (wire) with `const` assertions on size and field offsets, a `ver` field, explicit zeroed reserved fields covered by the CRC, golden vectors and an N/N-1 decode lane. Only `otap-sys` may contain `unsafe`; it exports owning types (`Arena`, `SlotRun`, `InFlight<Slot>`, `Ring`, `Op`, `SlotPage`, `NsHandle`) and probes `io_uring_probe` and `UBLK_U_CMD_GET_FEATURES` at startup to build the opcode allowlist. Traits are synchronous and reactor-local unless marked; nothing on the data path takes a lock, performs an RPC or touches a futex. Sketches are abridged to the fields and methods the contracts depend on; the record holds the full forms.

### 7.6 nvfail: the per-volume state machine and the client presentation

| State | Event | Action | Next |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online(idle) | first write after idle | intent slot (precise mode) or nothing (conservative), then ack | Online(active) |
| Online(active) | two CPs without a write | clear the bit at the CP | Online(idle) |
| Online(active) | clean shutdown or UPS low battery | final CP, `run_state = CleanShutdown` | Offline(clean) |
| Offline(clean) | boot | export | Online(idle) |
| Online(active) | power loss or crash | nothing (volatile) | Offline(dirty) |
| Offline(dirty) | boot; posture `Hold` | verify summaries (rewind on failure, cause `TornCp`), set `nvfail[v]`, superblock before export, alert; the engine answers EIO; `nbladed` presents per protocol | NvFailed |
| Offline(dirty) | boot; posture `Fence` | bump `rollback_gen`, alert, export under the new identity | Online(idle) |
| Replica(applied K) | promoted; posture `Hold`, or lag beyond `nvfail_force_lag`, or `Unknown`, or `force_hold` | `nvfail[v]` with cause `Promote`; nothing acknowledged | NvFailed (revertible while every volume is held) |
| Replica(applied K) | promoted; posture `Fence` and provably not idle | bump `rollback_gen`, alert, export under the new identity | Online(idle) |
| Replica(applied K) | promoted; provably idle, or posture `Off` | export | Online(idle) |
| NvFailed | `otapctl volume nvfail-clear <vol> [--restore <snap>]` | bump `rollback_gen`; optional rollback; re-export per `LunIdentity.policy` | Online(idle) |
| NvFailed | `Revert` (old owner returned intact, every volume held) | drop the promotion; resync from base | Replica |
| NvFailed | any I/O | EIO at the engine (defence in depth) | NvFailed |

The client presentation per protocol and class is the table in 6.6; the mechanisms are: block, unmap from every TPG and ACL (25h/00h on live paths), then on clear either a remap under the same serial (`SameIdentity`) or a new `vpd_unit_serial` and LUN number while `export_count == 0` (`NewIdentity`); NFS, export withheld, then re-exported with `fsid = <uuid>-g<rollback_gen>`; SMB, `smbcontrol close-share` and `available = no`, or an smbd restart under `Fence`; S3, versitygw stopped or restarted. ALUA Unavailable and Standby are reserved for `stop` and ordinary failovers.

### 7.7 Invariants every crate's property tests encode

1. A seq-prefix of acknowledged writes is exactly an image a power cut could have produced; no acknowledged write is lost across a daemon re-attach (`otap-arena`, `otap-index`, the checker).
2. Every logical change, including a free, carries a birth; `diff(base)` over the map equals the union of CP write sets for `cp > base` with later writes winning, over random snapshot pairs including discards, resizes and GC between the pair (`otap-map`).
3. Every byte of every structure is covered by a CRC; a layout change fails to compile; encode, decode and re-encode are byte-identical; N decodes N-1's vectors (`otap-format`, `otap-stream`).
4. Every data bio on a parity pool is stripe-aligned and stripe-sized, including metadata, superblock and intent writes (the dm-log-writes log proves it).
5. A torn superblock or data stripe falls back to CP N-1 intact; a torn CP on a `flush-opaque` backstore falls back to N-2 intact (dm-flakey).
6. A dirty boot engages nvfail for exactly the checker's set of volumes with unflushed acknowledged writes; a clean shutdown never trips it; a volume idle for two CPs never appears in precise mode.
7. A held promotion acknowledges nothing and writes no data CP; `Revert` happens only before any acknowledgement on the survivor; no two nodes acknowledge writes for one SVM in overlapping real time (`otap-sim`, madsim, Apalache in Phase B).
8. The replica is at a sender CP boundary after every `Committed`; a BLAKE3 over the pinned root at a common CP is equal on both sides; a `DeltaBegin` with a known `cp_seq` and a different `cp_id` discards exactly one partial; duplicate applies equal zero and resends equal the duplicate count (the loopback harness).
9. Every snapshot retained on the sender and younger than the base exists on the receiver with identical content after catch-up (ordered increments).
10. The slot check is correct under loom against a concurrent writer; the control plane performs zero syscalls on the data path over 60 s of I/O (`perf trace`).
11. 1,000 SVM create and destroy cycles leak no namespace and no mount; the pins exist on the host after `unshare --fork` returns; the view is invisible on the host; a snapshot exported with its own `fsid` resolves to the snapshot's data, never the live volume's.
12. The reconciler, killed mid-apply 100 times, converges to the desired hash and never deletes a device; `queue/write_cache == "write back"` and `fua == 1` are asserted before every LUN link; no data LUN is ever mapped at LUN 0.

### 8.4 Test strategy

**Format freeze.** `otap-format` and `otap-stream` are frozen in week 1: `repr(C)` zerocopy views with `const` size and offset assertions; proptest with 100,000 cases per structure per merge and one million nightly, asserting byte-identical round trips and full CRC coverage; golden vectors with a blake3 manifest, so any semantic change needs a new vector and a minor bump in the same commit; an N/N-1 decode lane; cargo-fuzz on both parsers for one CPU-hour each nightly.

**Crash consistency.** dm-log-writes over a loop device (or md over loops) under `Backstore`; `dmsetup message <dev> 0 mark cp<N>` after every superblock completion; xfstests' `replay-log` (built from source in the CI image) replays to every mark and to 1,000 random bio boundaries, then the recovery path runs read-only: the max-select superblock verifies, the image equals a seq-prefix of the checker's acknowledged writes, and XFS inside a NAS volume mounts clean at marks. The log also proves every data bio is stripe-aligned and stripe-sized, which replaces the "zero member reads" test that needs a real array. dm-flakey (`drop_writes`, `corrupt_bio_byte` during a commit window) proves the fall-back to N-1 and the two-CP deferred reuse. An in-process fault injector on `Backstore` (reorder, drop after a point, duplicate) runs per merge; the kernel lane runs nightly.

**Simulation.** `otap-sim` drives the engine core against fake `Backstore`, `FrontDoor`, `Clock` and `Net` with a seeded scheduler choosing which pending completion fires next and crash points between any two trait calls; 10,000 seeds per merge, one million nightly; invariants 1, 2, 6, 7 and 8 of 7.7. madsim covers `otap-ctl` and `nbladed`'s control logic behind a `Platform` trait that fakes configfs, netns and systemd. turmoil covers the stream's `TokioTcp` test transport (partition, duplicate, reorder across connections).

**CI without storage hardware.** A Debian 13 cloud image under QEMU/KVM: 4 vCPUs, 6 GB, 64 huge pages (a 128 MiB arena; every size is a profile value), eight 2 GiB sparse qcow2 disks as virtio-blk, `modprobe ublk_drv ublks_max=256`. Performance numbers are recorded, never asserted; the only timing gates are functional (re-attach under 2 s, SVM start under 5 s). One physical lab box (IT-mode HBA, an mdadm RAID6, 10GbE) runs the nightly performance lane and the five real power cuts; the development PC is not target hardware and is never used for either.

**Loopback replication.** Two aggregates in one `dbladed`; a `DeltaSource` on aggregate 0 feeds the real `TlsTcp` stream to 127.0.0.1:7430, where the receiver reactor of the same process lands into aggregate 1's `DeltaSink` through the CP assembler with shadow roots and `ShipApply`; fio writes while the harness snapshots every 20 CPs. Checks: after every `Committed`, a blake3 over the pinned root at the common CP is equal on both sides; `kill -9` of the whole process at 50 random points, including between staging and the superblock write, yields exactly one discarded partial by `cp_id` mismatch; `ss -K` on the connection resumes from the chunk bitmap with duplicates equal to resends; a flipped byte produces `Abort(Corrupt)` and a clean resend. The same harness drives `dump --since <snap>` to a file and `restore` into aggregate 1, which is the user-visible v0.1 feature; the TCP path is a test vehicle until Phase B.

**ESXi 8.0 U3, NFSv3.** A datastore SVM export with `fsid=<uuid>`, `rw,sync,no_root_squash`; NFS 4.1 datastores are tested, not recommended. After nvfail the datastore is inaccessible until `nvfail clear`, and then stale: power off every VM, unmount, remount by server and path, re-register. Budget twenty minutes in vCenter. **Proxmox VE 9.1, single path (the default build).** `pvesm add iscsi` and `pvesm add lvm --base ... --shared 1`; one LIF, no multipath.
